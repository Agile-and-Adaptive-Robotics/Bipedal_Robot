Session 1, **easteregg2**, 2 November 2026. Committee review index, not a manuscript revision or an automatic training release.

Nominal S4 and quarter-strength S5 repeats are byte-identical to their original traces and models. The next 35%-lift quarter-strength case fails with two qualifying swings per foot. “PASS” below always names the tested scope. An electrical current/clock proof is not body acceptance; a body result belongs to its exact saved controller and apparatus. Neural-only mechanisms do not need a fictitious body test, and supported/reduced models do not need free 3D walking to complete their declared design scope.

## Applicable FSA, scaling, and equation checks

1. **Graded signal functions:** declare current/voltage domains, reversals, operating points, signed typed fan-in, membrane/encoder timing, saturation, and actual consuming arrays. Conductance shunting makes multiple inputs nonlinear; an isolated equilibrium target is not an arbitrary-amplitude linear gain. See [Szczecinski, Hunt, and Quinn (2017)](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neurorobotics/articles/10.3389/fnbot.2017.00037/full), [graded design](D:/GitHub/Bipedal_Robot/Code/MuJoCo_SNS/spinal/fsa_design_20261101/GRADED_GAIT_DESIGN.md), and the [worked actual 0.375 µS local-pool example](D:/GitHub/Bipedal_Robot/Code/MuJoCo_SNS/spinal/fsa_design_20261101/DEFENSE_CONTROLLER_MATH_20261102.md).
2. **Nonlinear NaP/Ca rhythm mechanisms:** preserve their ionic/gate equations and fit/check rhythm and interactions under the intended inputs. Their gates are not GLIF threshold adaptation. A smaller intrinsic time constant changes biological dynamics; sparse computation/preallocation changes wall time only.
3. **Pure GLIF Table 2:** apply the activation/rate, threshold/adaptation, bias, membrane, synaptic-decay, and event-amplitude steps only to compatible models. The implementation of [Szczecinski et al. (2020)](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neurorobotics/articles/10.3389/fnbot.2020.577804/full) preserves a literal unit-gain negative result: N16 gain 1.12148 fails the registered 3% tolerance. The separate conductance-aware extension passes its tested excitatory constant-threshold scope; adaptive/inhibitory counterparts are not implemented. [Spiking design record](D:/GitHub/Bipedal_Robot/Code/MuJoCo_SNS/spinal/fsa_design_20261101/SPIKING_DESIGN_RECORD.md).
4. **Analytic phase primitives:** phase rate/reset, waveform centers/widths, and signed recruitment need their own tests. [Di Russo et al. (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1088/1741-2552/acfdcc) use phase oscillators and five raised-cosine primitives; Syn6 K6 uses a different learned basis and neuronal dynamics. Do not apply a passive-membrane recipe as though those abstractions were identical.
5. **Time and size:** separate source biological time, physical time, and computation time. The 0.559 s cached stride/fourth-harmonic 7.1556 Hz context gives a declared 10° per-encoder budget, implemented at 2 ms with a 3.89623 ms discrete-aware encoder constant. It is not a universal human maximum or whole-loop bandwidth proof. Mechanical scaling depends on inertia, damping, stiffness, movement speed, and constraints, not mass alone; [Sutton et al.'s 2021 preprint](https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-153297/v1) provides the mechanics-space motivation. Applicable clock/unit adapters and finite-rate limitations remain model-specific.

Hunt's four-part instructional method is cited as **A. J. Hunt, “Real-time control of dynamic walking in a dog-like robot,” INRC2 Meeting, 15 February 2019**, as supplied by Ben. Hunt A–D calibration and physical curriculum stages 1–5 are distinct indices. Corrected stage 4 requires ground-loaded harness-supported stepping, not another suspended-air test. The current Hunt landing encoder emits an event after actual unloading; Syn6's retained weak heel-IN test is a different input policy.
