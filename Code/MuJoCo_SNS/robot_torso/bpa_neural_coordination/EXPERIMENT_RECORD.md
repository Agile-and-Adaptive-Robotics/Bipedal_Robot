## Scope of the first bounded experiment

Audit and test the actuator interface in an **isolated antagonistic one-joint MuJoCo fixture**, with a small SNS-Toolbox network and prescribed alternating PF input. This is not the full CAD robot, not an autonomous CPG, and not a standing/stepping or five-stage-curriculum pass. No production runner, winner, or dissertation source is changed.

The network must determine motor-neuron outputs through ordinary synaptic inputs. No independent torque allocation or cancellation of MN synaptic currents is allowed. Position and velocity encoders provide sensory feedback; matched ablations silence their inputs without changing the mechanical model or descending PF stimulus.

The existing `bpa_muscle.py` surface and Fmax scaling provide the static forward law. A bounded inverse maps requested tension to pressure. Actual chamber pressure is a separate state with explicitly assumed fill/vent time constants and delay. Held-out force-gain/loading-direction changes are **synthetic robustness probes**, not identified pneumatic properties. Static port agreement is not a new experimental validation of Ben's characterization.

### Actuator and numerical checks

- 216 static pressure/length cases across 10, 20, and 40 mm BPAs agree with the existing `BPAMuscle` implementation to **2.38e−8 N**. Source coefficients agree exactly with `Code\Matlab\Functions\festo_lookup_coeffs.json`. This is port agreement, not independent experimental validation.
- For feasible positive forces generated by the forward law, the analytic inverse recovers pressure within **7.17e−12 kPa**. Infeasible tension requests saturate and are flagged. Zero target tension commands zero pressure. The declared contraction domain is enforced; extension beyond resting length is not silently extrapolated.
- At a requested static force T, series deformation is T/K. Substituting that deformation into the pressure-affine Festo surface gives the pressure inverse directly. No learned inverse or nested pressure optimizer is required for this particular static law with zero Xi0/Xi3.
- The preallocated Numba update matches the actual compiled SNS-Toolbox network over 2,000 input samples to **4.45e−16 mV**. Full MuJoCo traces differ by at most **8.33e−16 rad** in joint angle, **8.19e−15 mV** in neurons, **3.98e−13 kPa** in pressure, and **4.09e−12 N** in force between native and accelerated neural backends.
- A separate fresh-process replay of the saved configuration reproduced the original trace **bit-identically**. Warm repeated fresh-model replay also had maximum difference 0.0.

### Actual controller and mechanical fixture

There are **10 graded neurons and 10 conductance-based synapses**: two externally driven PF neurons, four position/velocity encoders, two MNs, and two reciprocal inhibitory interneurons. MN outputs use the ordinary SNS update and synapses. **There is no MN-current cancellation, independent torque allocator, direct-force tracking override, or hidden PID controller.** The PF input is a prescribed 0.5 Hz alternating current, so this is not an autonomous rhythm-generator test or a full Hunt training procedure.

Position and velocity encoders are labeled II/Ia in the prototype; they are rectified joint-state deviations from a fixed neutral posture, equivalent up to the fixture's fixed moment arms to actuator length/velocity signals. They are not calibrated biological spindle models, and no Ib force-feedback circuit is included yet. The output conversion maps normalized MN voltage to a requested tension, then converts that to a pressure command using the static inverse at current length. Actual pressure is updated separately before the force calculation. Fixed moment-arm transmission is **±30 mm**, rather than a fitted spatial muscle path.

The MuJoCo 2.3.7 model has one fixed-base knee-like hinge, a 1 kg shank, gravity along −y, explicit joint damping, implicitfast, and 2 ms physics/neural steps. A protective positive-flexion limit starts 0.05 rad before extension neutral. No recorded trial hyperextends. This fixture is not Gait2392, the CAD robot, a virtual-walker import, or a test of bipedal balance/contact.

Synthetic actuator configuration: one 20 mm BPA per antagonist, rest length 0.30 m, maximum contraction fraction 0.25, nonmuscle route length 0.06 m, finite series stiffness 200 kN/m, and maximum pressure 620 kPa. These are fixture parameters, not a packaging/calibration claim for the CAD robot. All characterization coefficients remain unchanged.

### Training and held-out conditions

Eighteen candidate neural configurations were evaluated on two declared training conditions: nominal pressure dynamics and a slower, slightly weaker actuator. Candidate parameters were paired position/velocity synaptic conductance and descending PF amplitude, phase, and constant bias offset. The same gain is used for corresponding antagonist feedback synapses. No separate left/right body was simulated, so this is not a bilateral-symmetry validation.

The selected neural parameters were frozen before three held-out actuator/disturbance conditions were evaluated. The saved `FROZEN_CONFIGURATION.json` contains the exact parameters. Each feedback ablation used the same frozen configuration, initial state, PF input, and mechanical/actuator condition; the four sensory input currents were set to zero. The open-loop comparator was **not separately retuned**, so these results demonstrate the contribution of those feedback paths to this configuration, not superiority to every possible open-loop controller.

| Case | Pressure fill/vent τ | Delay | Force gain | Synthetic loading-direction gain | Joint-angle RMSE, feedback on/off |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nominal | 40/60 ms | 4 ms | 1.00 | 0 | **3.81° / 11.53°** |
| Held-out slower/weaker | 100/120 ms | 16 ms | 0.82 | +0.08 | **5.59° / 14.77°** |
| Held-out faster/stronger | 25/35 ms | 2 ms | 1.15 | −0.08 | **3.81° / 12.02°** |
| Held-out external pulse | 75/100 ms | 12 ms | 0.90 | +0.06 | **4.90° / 13.77°** |

RMSE uses seconds 2–8 of each 8 s run against the same desired 0.5 Hz, 0.12 rad amplitude angle waveform centered at 0.35 rad. Feedback reduces RMSE by **62–68%** in these specific matched tests, but nonzero offsets/amplitude errors remain. The held-out pulse is an additional 0.18 N·m for 120 ms at t=4 s. The training slower case used fill/vent 65/80 ms, delay 8 ms, force gain 0.95, and no loading-direction term.

The loading-direction term is an **assumed phenomenological perturbation**, not identified BPA hysteresis: a filtered tanh of actual length velocity modulates force gain. Its 80 ms filter and 0.004 m/s velocity scale are assumed. Positive velocity denotes lengthening, negative denotes shortening. This synthetic test does not establish biological eccentric-force behavior or reproduce a measured pneumatic hysteresis loop.

### Measured execution time

For an identical 2,000-sample neural replay, native SNS took 0.06697 s versus 0.004246 s with the warmed Numba update: **15.8× neural-kernel acceleration**. Complete 8 s fixture runs took **1.081 s native versus 0.808 s accelerated**, or **1.34× end-to-end acceleration**. Those complete timings include Python orchestration, pressure/force calculation, logging, and per-step timing instrumentation; compilation and imports are excluded.

A separate fresh process took approximately 2.86 s for imports and 1.58 s for its first 8 s run including fixture setup and initial disk-cache dispatch. The existing on-disk Numba cache was used. An uncached compilation benchmark was not performed. These timings apply to this small one-joint fixture and must not be extrapolated to full-walker throughput.
