gastrocnemius muscle A constant coordination between the legs is required to maintain stability during human locomotion. Research suggests that lower interlimb coordination may be mediated by spinal interneuronal circuits under supraspinal control. While such circuits are well documented in animals, evidence suggesting the existence of these in humans has only recently emerged; afferent feedback arising from receptors in the soleus (SOL) muscle on one side affects the activation of the contralateral SOL by eliciting a so-called short-latency crossed response (SLCR). The aim of this thesis was to provide further insight into interlimb coordination, investigating the interlimb neural pathways connecting gastrocnemii muscles during human locomotion.
