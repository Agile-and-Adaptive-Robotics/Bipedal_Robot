[ABSTRACT - Zotero local]
This chapter focuses on the key principles of biomechanics and motor control that underlie motor behaviors. It describes how the transformation of neural activity into individual muscle forces depends on both intrinsic neuromuscular properties and muscle function. The chapter examines how the effects of activating a muscle in producing a behavior depend on body structure, interaction with environmental forces, and behavioral context. It focuses on the transformation from muscle force to the body forces and movements that generate motor behavior. Classical descriptions of muscle function are based on anatomical arrangements describing muscles as having singular functions. Then the chapter discusses on the coordination of multiple muscles to generate functional movements and addresses the broad question of motor control from the perspective of biomechanics. It argues throughout that, in both vertebrates and invertebrates, muscle multi-functionality versus specialization drives the relative complexity of neural versus muscular adaptations in generating motor behavior.
