# Group-II pathway sign: bounded source audit

Session 1, easteregg2. Static literature/model inspection only; no new simulations or parameter changes.

## The apparent contradiction

An inhibitory arrow from a II-labelled unit is not sufficient to infer inhibition of the muscle supplying the signal. Record the sensed muscle, receptor calculation, adapter slope/offset, every intervening relay, final target, and operating phase. Distinguish central RG/PF timing pathways from homonymous MN stretch feedback. An excitatory primary afferent can recruit an inhibitory interneuron; a reduced model may collapse that relay into one effective inhibitory connection.

## Direct primary evidence: Hunt, Szczecinski, and Quinn (2017)

[Development and Training of a Neural Controller for Hind Leg Walking in a Dog Robot](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neurorobotics/articles/10.3389/fnbot.2017.00018/full), §§2.2.1–2.2.2 and Figure 10, explicitly separates local motor feedback from whole-leg timing. Hip-flexor Ia/II oppose the extensor timing branch, and hip-extensor II opposes the flexor timing branch. The paper calls the recipients Extensor Interneuron and Flexor Interneuron; retain those labels rather than silently replacing them with the newer rat model's RG/PF half-center endpoints. Increasing hip-flexor stretch and unloading ankle extensors support the stance-to-swing transition; increasing hip-extensor II supports the opposite transition. These are central coordination pathways, not a universal prescription that II inhibits its own MN. The hardware feedback calculation is a simplified velocity/force/length encoding, which must not be assumed identical to AnimatLab's artificial spindle implementation.

## Exact public biarticular rat source

Author source: [Biarticular_net_Standalone.asim](https://github.com/vipamol/Rat-SNS-design-and-tuning/blob/main/Biarticular_net/Biarticular_net_Standalone.asim). This is the imported public 2020 precursor, not verified final-2023 release. The static extraction is `runs\II_SOURCE_WIRING_AUDIT.json`, generated by `audit_ii_sources.py`.

| Source signal and II-labelled unit | Actual downstream target | Synapse reversal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| LH iliopsoas receptor → LH_HipZ-FLX II | EXT RG and EXT hip/knee/ankle PF | −70 mV |
| LH biceps femoris anterior receptor → LH_HipZ-Ext II | FLX RG and FLX hip/knee/ankle PF | −70 mV |
| LH tibialis anterior receptor → LH_TA-FLX II | Own LH TA MN | −10 mV |

Thus this source contains both antagonist central inhibition and homonymous motor excitation. The actual II adapter slopes are positive, with muscle-specific offsets on the left. Right-side adapters retain different defaults, and RH TA-II has no outgoing connection in this file. The model therefore cannot support a claim that every author, every side, or every II pathway shares one sign.

Functional interpretation, not a new simulation result: inhibiting the extensor half-center can release the mutually inhibited flexor half-center, potentially facilitating the muscle whose flexor spindle supplied the signal. The converse applies to the extensor pathway. Such disinhibition depends on the operating state, tonic drive, saturation, and the remaining circuit; an inhibitory arrow alone does not prove its final motor effect.

## Artificial spindle signal is not just a label

Official [AnimatLab LinearHillStretchReceptor source](https://www.animatlab.com/SDK/_linear_hill_stretch_receptor_8cpp_source.html), CalculateTension, calculates Ia rate from **series-element length** and II rate from **parallel-element length**, each multiplied by a discharge constant. In particular, its Ia output is not simply muscle-length velocity. Both signal dynamics depend on the receptor's mechanics and its adapter. The same equations are transcribed in the current accelerated `virtual_walker_20261031/native_kernel.py`.

Consequently, a nominal II waveform can shift when series compliance, loading, receptor parameters, or route geometry change. A transplanted controller needs measured source-signal phase and scaling checks. In a biarticular muscle, both joint motions affect total route length; a single joint's flexion/extension label is insufficient to predict spindle phase.

## Why two apparent feedback signs can agree

This is a local kinematic example, not an attribution of author intent. For one DOF and constant opposite moment-arm magnitudes, let

`l_F = l_F0 + r_F q`, and `l_E = l_E0 − r_E q`, with `r_F,r_E > 0`.

Then `k(l_F − l_F0) = −k(r_F/r_E)(l_E − l_E0)`.

Exciting a flexor output with its own length can therefore have the same linear feedback component as inhibiting that output with antagonist length, after the reference offsets and moment-arm ratio are included. This does not make every circuit or conductance synapse equivalent: thresholds, shunting, nonlinear moment arms, compliant tendons, biarticular routes, and network dynamics can destroy the equivalence. Central reciprocal disinhibition is an additional circuit mechanism, separate from this algebra.

## Physiology and limits of attribution

[Bannatyne et al. (2009)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19047211/) experimentally identified both excitatory and inhibitory feline intermediate-zone interneurons receiving group-I/II input, with projections into motor nuclei and other spinal regions. Therefore, II does not specify a unique final pathway sign.

Szczecinski, Hunt, and Quinn's [functional-subnetwork paper (2017)](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neurorobotics/articles/10.3389/fnbot.2017.00037/full), §§2.1 and 3, explicitly treats sensor-to-neural mappings and neural addition/subtraction/modulation. It does not establish a universal mammalian II wiring rule; its worked locomotor application concerns insect-inspired control.

The [Deng 2019 paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6477610/) does not explicitly resolve the II-label/sign question in its searchable text. Do not substitute the newer precursor's wiring for a verified 2019 connection audit, or call that precursor the final-2023 model.

[Lutz's 2025 thesis record](https://pdxscholar.library.pdx.edu/open_access_etds/6989/) confirms adapting a rat SNS to Muscle Mutt and converting robot measurements into biological-style feedback. The full-text download returned 403 during this bounded check, so no specific II connection sign is attributed to Lutz here. No relevant accessible primary Micklem locomotor circuit/derivation was established; the available 2026 conference program is not sufficient evidence of its connectivity. These two model-specific attributions remain unverified.

SADb's II-excitatory/II-inhibitory entries and older repository rulings were used as search leads, not promoted to primary physiological evidence. In particular, DESIGN.md's older caution against adding a direct II→antagonist-MN inhibitory shortcut does not contradict the central RG/PF timing pathways documented above.
