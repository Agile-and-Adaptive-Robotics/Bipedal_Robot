247:OpenSim \citep{delp_opensim_2007, seth_opensim_2018}, distributed through the SimTK (Simulation Toolkit) repository, provides the biomechanical modeling side: musculoskeletal models with detailed bone geometry, muscle routing, and force-generating properties, along with tools for analyzing moment arms and isometric torque. These models are approximations with known limits rather than exact anatomies: muscle paths are interpolations over discrete attachment and wrapping data, and near the ends of a joint's range the routed paths of sparsely wrapped muscles can bunch up or pass through bone, an interference documented within Gait2392-derived routings themselves \citep{bolen_bipedal_2019}. Their geometry is accurate for a purpose, namely moment arms and torque predictions over the functional range of motion, and that is the standard against which the robot model of this program is compared. The same SimTK infrastructure hosts SCONE, an open-source package for predictive simulation and optimization of neuromuscular control of biological motion \citep{geijtenbeek_scone_2019}. AnimatLab \citep{cofer_animatlab_2010} provides a graphical environment in which physical bodies and synthetic neural systems are constructed and co-simulated; the AARL's prior walker (Section~\ref{sec:prior_walker}) was built in it. MuJoCo \citep{todorov_mujoco_2012} provides the physics-engine side: fast, accurate simulation of rigid-body dynamics with contacts, along with muscle-like actuators that include activation dynamics. MyoConverter bridges the biomechanical and physics-engine worlds by converting OpenSim musculoskeletal models to MuJoCo format with optimized muscle kinematics and kinetics \citep{caggiano_myosuite_2022, wang_myosim_2022}. Combined with the SNS-Toolbox's MuJoCo backend \citep{nourse_sns_2023}, these tools were used to convert the laboratory's modified OpenSim leg model to MuJoCo, couple it to a synthetic nervous system with Ia, Ib, and II proprioceptive channels plus contact sensors, and run the full neuromechanical loop faster than real time (Sections~\ref{sec:preliminary_sim_methods}--\ref{sec:preliminary_sim_results}). Simulation speed is the practical enabler: a single overnight run can sweep thousands of parameter combinations or execute hundreds of simulated gait cycles per condition, throughput that no human-subject or animal protocol could match, and that transforms lesion and stimulation studies from bespoke demonstrations into statistically powered experiments.
