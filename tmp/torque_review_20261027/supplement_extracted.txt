1.4 Contact and Release Angles of the Wrapped Route
The route-elimination rule that schedules the wrapped extensor route is a route-topology state machine
rather than a shortest-path solver: the fully flexed route carries every candidate contact, and each contact is
released at the first more-extended knee angle at which the straight chord between its surviving neighbors
can replace it without entering the exclusion envelopes. This section states the calculation in generic terms,
so that it applies to any wrapped actuator route on a rotating joint pair; the extensor route of Table S5 gives
the cam-route example; its release schedule follows the same rule on the cam geometry.
The route is stored as a fixed number of rows p1, . . . , p9: rows p1 through p5 are femur-frame points,
rows p6 through p9 are tibia-frame points, and the segment from p5 to p6 crosses from one body frame into
the other. The endpoints p1 and p9 are the actuator origin and insertion, and they are always active. Each
interior row is either active, a genuine wrap contact, or eliminated; an eliminated femur-side row repeats
the preceding active femur row, and an eliminated tibia-side row repeats the neighboring active tibia row,
so the route keeps a fixed size and its segment chain crosses the joint exactly once. Let Apθkq Ď t2, . . . ,8u
denote the active interior rows at knee angle θk. The sweep over the design grid, here 100 poses from
´120˝ to 10˝ at a spacing of 1.31˝, proceeds from full flexion toward extension, each pose inherits the
active set of the previous pose, and rows are only ever removed, soA shrinks monotonically as the knee
extends.
