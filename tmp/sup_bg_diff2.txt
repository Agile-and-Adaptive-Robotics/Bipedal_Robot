+A wide range of actuation technologies have been explored in robotics, including electric motors, hydraulic systems, smart-material actuators, and artificial muscles \citep{he_mechanism_2020, liang_comparative_2020}. Electric motors are widely used due to their controllability and maturity, but they are limited by thermal constraints, gearing requirements, and reduced compliance compared to muscle-like actuators \citep{veale_towards_2016, suzumori_trends_2018}. Hydraulic actuators offer exceptionally high power density and have enabled highly dynamic legged systems, but they remain relatively heavy, less efficient than biological muscle, and require a dedicated fluid infrastructure \citep{suzumori_trends_2018}.
+
+Among smart-material actuators, dielectric elastomer actuators (DEAs), a class of electronic electroactive polymers, contract by a different mechanism: an applied voltage squeezes a soft polymer film between compliant electrodes through electrostatic stress, and the thinning and radial spreading of the film produces strains that can exceed biological muscle \citep{madden_artificial_2004}. Comparative studies report high specific work and good bandwidth for DEAs \citep{liang_comparative_2020}, but kilovolt-range driving voltages, electromechanical instability, and the compliant-electrode requirement have kept them out of legged robots. Thermally activated artificial muscles reach the most extreme performance figures of any technology surveyed by \citet{liang_comparative_2020}: twisted-fiber muscles support loads more than one hundred times those of a biological muscle of the same length and weight, with output stresses of \qty{22}{\mega\pascal}. Their activation route, however, is shared with shape-memory alloys, whose heating is fast but whose cooling is limited by heat transfer away from the element, and that asymmetry sets the cycle time.

+\section{Human Lower-Limb Biomechanics and the OpenSim Benchmark}\label{sec:bg_biomechanics}

+Designing a biomimetic leg requires a quantitative benchmark of human muscle--joint behavior. The human knee is itself a complex joint: its instantaneous center of rotation migrates posteriorly with flexion, a behavior captured by four-bar linkage descriptions of the joint \citep{romero_four-bar_2020, steele_biomimetic_2018}. Throughout this dissertation, Gait2392 serves as the reference against which the robot's muscle routing, moment arms, and torque capabilities are designed and judged.

+\section{Computational Neuroscience of Locomotor Circuits}\label{sec:bg_compneuro}
 
-The biomechanical model consisted of nine rigid bodies --- pelvis, thigh, shank, foot, and toe segments --- connected by sagittal-plane hinge joints with human-like rotational capabilities and actuated by antagonistic pairs of Hill-type muscles at the hip, knee, and ankle. The toe joint, the author's addition to the Li design, carried a passive spring instead of a muscle pair, simplifying the system. The mechanics followed the suspended bipedal walking model of \citet{li_bipedal_2017}, whose CPGs were entrained by heel and toe mechanosensory receptors and hip-angle position sensors standing in for the group-I loading, cutaneous, and hip-joint afferents of the biological scheme --- muscle afferents were not modeled --- and it is worth noting that the Li model required support against falling: a system with only planar joints cannot actively balance side to side.
+Computational neuroscience expresses hypotheses about locomotor circuitry as executable models. A locomotor model is a system of coupled differential equations in which every element of the schematic corresponds to an identified neural population or pathway; the wiring diagram is not an illustration of the model, it is the model. The founding hypothesis is Brown's half-center: alternating flexor and extensor activity arises from two reciprocally inhibitory central populations with opposing bias \citep{brown_intrinsic_1911}. Modern instantiations of the half-center endow each neuron with persistent sodium channels, whose slow inactivation terminates each burst so that reciprocal inhibition switches the populations without any phasic input \citep{deng_neuromechanical_2019}. Rhythmogenesis, in this tradition, is a property of the intrinsic membrane dynamics, not of a clock elsewhere in the network.
 
-The synthetic nervous network was built from non-spiking, leaky-integrator neurons and followed the two-layer CPG organization of \citet{deng_neuromechanical_2019}. A rhythm-generator layer set the timing of the system; a pattern-formation layer reshaped the timing signals into phase-appropriate activation of antagonist muscle groups; and a sensorimotor layer containing Renshaw-cell-like recurrent inhibition drove the motor neurons. The sensorimotor layer received Ia (length and velocity) and Ib (tension) afferent feedback from the muscles, but the feedback gains were never systematically tuned, and no ground-contact signals were modeled. Every applied current, synaptic weight, and muscle-tension parameter was set by hand: each was adjusted by trial and error, one at a time, until the three layers oscillated in phase and the motor neurons produced stepping-like activation of the muscle pairs. That hand-tuning consumed the bulk of the project's effort, left no record of the sensitivity of the behavior to individual parameters, and did not generalize when new feedback pathways were added --- limitations that directly motivated the systematic, verification-first tuning approach of Chapter~\ref{ch:futurework}.
+The two-level organization described in Section~\ref{sec:bg_neural} is the field's current working architecture, and its models account for specific electrophysiological observations: the fictive-locomotion deletions reproduced by \citet{rybak_modelling_2006}, the genetically identified interneuron classes mapped onto the model by \citet{rybak_organization_2015}, and the organization of circuits controlling two-joint muscles reviewed by \citet{shevtsova_modeling_2016}. Coupling such circuits to musculoskeletal models shows how afferent feedback shapes CPG output \citep{markin_afferent_2010} and how biomechanics and sensory feedback regularize the behavior of different CPG architectures \citep{deng_biomechanical_2022}. Physiologically plausible models of human locomotion use the same machinery to dissect the relative contributions of reflexes and CPGs, with locomotor primitives, Ia, II, and Ib pathways, and Renshaw cells represented explicitly \citep{dirusso_investigating_2023}, and recent models address interlimb coordination under abrupt perturbations such as the sudden loss of ground support \citep{shinohara_mechanisms_2025}.
 
+For a mechanical engineer, the productive way to read this literature is as a controls tradition in which the block diagram is the controller itself: each block is an identifiable neural population with conductance-based dynamics, and each connection is a synaptic conductance with a physiologically interpretable weight. The connection schematics published with these models are therefore worth reading as carefully as their equations. The controller developed in Chapter~\ref{ch:sns} comes from this tradition: its two-layer organization follows the rat hindlimb model of \citet{deng_neuromechanical_2019}, and its verification tests are drawn from the deletion and stimulation phenomena that the models of \citet{rybak_modelling_2006} and the air-stepping experiments of \citet{selionov_tonic_2009} explain.

 \section{Testing Neuromechanical Hypotheses on Robots: The Ethical Case}\label{sec:bg_ethics}
 
-The experiments this research program is designed to enable --- deleting a muscle from a limb, blocking a sensory pathway, lesioning part of a rhythm generator, applying tonic stimulation to spinal circuits --- cannot be performed on healthy humans. Invasive intervention on the human neuromuscular system is limited to clinical populations, is tightly constrained by ethics review, and permits neither the within-subject control nor the reversibility that neuromechanical hypotheses demand. The same experiments in animals also carry real moral cost, and their justification is governed by the Three Rs framework: replacement of animal subjects with non-animal methods, reduction of the numbers required, and refinement of the remaining procedures \citep{russell_principles_1959, tannenbaum_russell_2015}. A robot that implements a validated neuromechanical hypothesis is precisely such a replacement method: every deletion, lesion, and stimulation experiment that runs on the synthetic nervous system is an experiment that does not require a living subject, and the robot population can be duplicated exactly, so that ``subjects'' are statistically identical across conditions --- a form of reduction no breeding colony can offer. Refinement follows as well: the failure modes of these experiments damage hardware, not tissue.

-For a robot to serve as a replacement subject, however, it must first earn standing as a scientific model. Webb's influential analyses of bio-robotic methodology \citep{webb_robots_2001, webb_robots_2002} argue that a robot is a good model of biological behavior when the mechanism it implements is tested at the same level of organization as the target phenomenon --- matching dynamics, control structure, and sensory ecology rather than surface appearance. +The procedures behind that record are documented in films that remain publicly available. A \qty{50}{\minute} classroom recording posted by the UTHealth Houston School of Dentistry first shows an intact cat playing with its handler, righting itself when lowered and placing its paws accurately on the edge of a table, and then performs the decerebration for the class \citep{utherst_decerebrate_2025}: the animal is anesthetized with open-drop ether, more than the horse in the same demonstration series received, the carotid arteries are ligated and a tracheal cannula installed, and the skull is opened with a trephine before the brainstem is transected between the superior and inferior colliculi. The treadmill preparations that established locomotor neurophysiology are likewise preserved in the historical literature: \citet{stuart_reflections_2007} reproduce the arrangement of the high-decerebrate cat treadmill experiments, with electromyographic recording from the hindlimb muscles, from the original Russian-language reports \citep{severin_1967}.
+
+The honest accounting is murkier than the Three Rs framing alone suggests, because the models a robot implements are themselves products of animal experimentation. The half-center organization, the two-level rhythm and pattern architecture, and the length, force, and load signaling of the afferents were each established in preparations like the ones described above \citep{brown_intrinsic_1911, severin_1967, rybak_modelling_2006}, and the databases of afferent properties that parameterize sensory feedback are compiled from animal recordings \citep{bolen_sensory_2023}. A synthetic nervous system therefore replaces future experiments; it does not replace, and cannot repay, the animal work on which its architecture is built. Recognizing this inheritance sharpens rather than undermines the case for robotic subjects: the knowledge purchased at that cost is precisely what makes a robotic replacement scientifically meaningful, and the robot is the instrument that keeps that knowledge productive without adding to the ledger.
 
-The argument is ultimately bidirectional, completing the loop described in Section~\ref{sec:project}. Experiments that are unethical, impractical, or statistically unreachable in humans and animals become routine on the robot; the resulting insights into neuromechanical control feed back into biology, into the design of the next robot iteration, and directly into prosthetic and orthotic devices that face the same problem of controlling muscles-like actuators for human users, whose central pattern generator controllers are an active topic in lower-limb exoskeleton research \citep{duan_gait_2024, pery_benchmarking_2026}. The ethical case and the scientific case are the same case: the robot is the instrument that makes these experiments possible.
+For a robot to serve as a replacement subject, however, it must first earn standing as a scientific model. Webb's influential analyses of bio-robotic methodology \citep{webb_robots_2001, webb_robots_2002} argue that a robot is a good model of biological behavior when the mechanism it implements is tested at the same level of organization as the target phenomenon, matching dynamics, control structure, and sensory ecology rather than surface appearance. The validation chain of this dissertation is constructed to that standard: the actuator force model is validated against bench data (Section~\ref{sec:results_force}), the joint torque model against isometric measurements and human torque envelopes (Section~\ref{sec:results_torque}), and the neuromechanical controller can be verified against published tonic stimulation and deletion phenomena \citep{selionov_tonic_2009, rybak_modelling_2006} before any locomotion tuning is attempted. Only then can a robot experiment be read as evidence about the hypothesized biological circuit rather than about the robot.
