Abstract. This work details a neuromechanical model of a bipedal
robot based oﬀ simpliﬁed anatomy of the human lower body. The con-
troller consists of a neural network that replicates central pattern gen-
erators (CPGs) that take the form of rhythm generators (RGs), pattern
formation (PF) networks, and sensorimotor networks. The current model
is unable to walk on it’s own, however this work demonstrates that bio-
logically similar gait patterns can be generated through the use of CPGs.
Keywords: CPG · Biped · Animatlab
1 Introduction
Biomimetic robots attempt to replicate human biology to further research in
both robotics and anatomy. By replicating both the biomechanical properties
and neural systems of a human, researchers can formulate hypotheses and test
them in a controlled environment.
These robotics allow for continued research into CPGs and how humans use
them to generate smooth and continuous gait while encountering perturbations.
Literature on CPGs is numerous [2] and their implementation on robotics is
an active area of research [4]. However, most robot models that use CPGs use
simpliﬁed models of the neural networks to drive actuation.
This work takes that research and creates a model of a bipedal walker in the
neuromechanical modeling software Animatlab [1]. The Animatlab environment
allows researchers to construct physical models that are controlled by a synthetic
neural network. The simulation detailed in this work looks to creating a model
to be used on biomimmetic bipedal robots.
2 Methods
The neuromechanical model was developed in Animatlab. Animatlab is a 3D
graphical environment that allows user to construct biomechanical systems that
are actuated by synthetic neural systems (SNS). A bipedal humanoid biome-
chanical model was developed in Animatlab, whose physical structure is based

2.1 Biomechanical Model
The biomechanical model created simulates the simpliﬁed anatomy of the human
lower body. This model consists of nine bodies: pelvis, thighs, shanks, feet, and
toes (Figure 1a). Hinge joints between segments allow for rotation with capa-
bilities similar to human anatomy. The model is designed to only perform gait
in the sagittal plane and therefore does not require joints with more degrees of
freedom.
Attached to the body of the model are Hill muscle model actuators. Antag-
onistic muscles about each joint create rotation. The only joint not to contain
a pair of antagonistic muscles is the toe joint, which contains a spring instead
to increase compliance of the foot and to conserve energy when walking (Figure
1b).

CPGs are created from four coupled nonspiking neurons that oscillate periods
of activity. Two neurons serve as outputs to diﬀerent networks. When one output
neuron is driven high the other is driven low. Two integrator neurons are included

in the network to drive this oscillation of activation and inhibition. These CPG
networks take the form of a rhythm generator network and a pattern formation
network [7].
RG networks maintain the timing of the system. The RG network is the
highest network in the hierarchical structure and is based oﬀ of the ﬁndings of
Zhang et al. [8]. Two output neurons are connected through mutual excitation
and are attached via inhibitory synapses to interneurons. The structure of the
used RG network can be seen in Figure 2a.
PF networks help reshape the timing signals coming from the RG layer and
dictate the activity of the sensorimotor layer. The PF layer also controls muscle
synergies between the knee and ankle muscles, activating them with similar
timing.

A third simpliﬁed CPG network is included in the sensorimotor network
and oscillates activity of motor neurons that drive the muscles. This network is
made of two Renshall cells coupled by inhibitory synapses. The Renshall cells
provide feedback, along with Ia signals, Ib signals, and PF network output, to
the motor neurons. These motor neurons output their activity to muscles to
drive contraction and relaxation. Figure 3 details the connections of the used
sensorimotor network.
Muscles provide feedback through aﬀerent receptors. Both Ia and Ib signals
are used in the feedback for the sensorimotor network. The Ia signals cause
an interneuron to discharge at a rate that is proportional to the velocity of
stretch in the muscle. The Ib signals cause an interneuron to discharge at a rate

3 Results
The oscillating signals from the diﬀerent neural networks create a stepping pat-
tern in the bipedal biomechanical model. The RG, PF, and motor neurons os-
cillate their levels of activation once the simulation is initiated. Figure 4 shows
that each couple neuron pair switches its voltage with a frequency of 4 Hz. The
output from these neurons drive the activation of the muscles on the model. As
one muscle in an antagonistic muscle pair begins to contract, the other extends.
This creates rotation about the joint (Figure 5). These ﬁgures show the results
from the left sided limbs, which are mirrored for the right side limbs. The model
is also suspended in the air. When the model is lowered to a platform below it,
it is unable to use the gait patterns to generate forward motion.
4 Discussion
The results show that a neural network comprised of a rhythm generator layer,
pattern formation layer, and sensorimotor layer can be used to actuate and drive
a bipedal model in a manner similar to gait. All layers of the network activate

at frequencies that are in phase with one another and the motor neuron outputs
properly actuate the muscles that drive the physical model.
The timing of the oscillation is faster than typical human gait, which is ap-
proximately 1 Hz [5]. The current model has a gait frequency of 4 Hz. This quick
timing frequency can be adjusted by altering the activation and deactivation val-
ues for the sodium channels.
As the model moves, all joints move to their maximum and minimum po-
sitions. This abrupt collision in the model can be corrected by adjusting the
stimulus tension values for the individual muscles. Muscles are stimulated by
the motor neurons and output a tension that is dictated by a polynomial curve
in Animatlab. By adjusting the values of this polynomial curve, the high ten-
sion values of the muscles can be lowered so that the joints don’t reach their
maximum and minimum values as quickly.
5 Conclusion
This work demonstrates the construction of a bipedal walker that is controlled
with a 2 layer CPG hierarchy. The rhythm generator CPG network, located at

the top of the network hierarchy, is initiated by a stimulus of 10 nA for a duration
of 1 ms, and creates a timing system for the rest of the networks. The pattern
formation networks take in the output from the rhythm generator to create step
timing frequency for the sensory motor networks. The sensory motor networks
take in the pattern formation output as well as aﬀerent signals from the muscles
to modulate step timing. The signals from the sensory motor network drive pair
of antagonistic muscles about a hip, knee, and ankle joint. Muscle tension and
velocity feedback into the sensory motor network.
The model is able to generate gait like stepping motions, however when low-
ered to the ground, it is unable to walk. Walking can be generated with further
tuning of neural and muscle parameters, as well as implementation of more feed-
back systems. By adjusting the activation and deactivation of the sodium channel
parameters, the model will more closely resemble the kinematics of human gait,
which will aid it while walking. By implementing more feedback systems, such
as heel and toe contact, the model will be able to modulate its stepping motion.
Contact sensors will help the neural network know when swing and stance are
necessary, by reinforcing stance when the model is supporting all of its weight on
one leg, while then reinforcing swing motions as the force on the contact sensor
begins to decrease.
