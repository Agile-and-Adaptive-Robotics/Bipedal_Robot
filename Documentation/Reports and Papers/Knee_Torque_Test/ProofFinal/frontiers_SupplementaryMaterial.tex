\subsection{Route-Redesign Objectives and Detailed Geometry}\label{app:route_redesign_details}

The flexor and extensor routes use different terms in the common objective of Equation~\eqref{eq:opt_cost}:
\begin{align}
J_{\mathrm{flx}} =\;& 10^{5}J_{\text{worst}} + 10^{3}J_{\text{torque}} + 10^{-2}J_{\text{shape}} + J_{\text{off}} + J_{\text{rest}} + J_{\text{strain,hi}} \nonumber\\
& + J_{\text{strain,lo}} + J_{\text{wrap}} + J_{\text{geom}} + J_{\text{len}} + J_{\text{anchor}},
\label{eq:opt_cost_flx}\\[4pt]
J_{\mathrm{ext}} =\;& 10^{5}J_{\text{worst}} + 10^{3}J_{\text{torque}} + J_{\text{over}} + J_{\text{rest}} + J_{\text{strain,hi}} \nonumber\\
& + J_{\text{strain,lo}} + J_{\text{contr}} + J_{\text{geom}} + J_{\text{len}}.
\label{eq:opt_cost_ext}
\end{align}
Here $J_{\text{shape}}$ penalizes torque-curve disagreement, $J_{\text{off}}$ off-axis torque, $J_{\text{rest}}$ infeasible resting length, $J_{\text{strain,hi}}$ and $J_{\text{strain,lo}}$ strain-limit violations, $J_{\text{wrap}}$ failure to release a wrap, $J_{\text{anchor}}$ attachment displacement, $J_{\text{over}}$ torque overshoot, and $J_{\text{contr}}$ excessive contraction. Geometric and length regularizers retain the seed neighborhood. The cam formulation additionally rewards worst-angle torque margin and penalizes abrupt changes in moment arm and displacement from the initial cam profile. These additions seek usable torque capacity while preserving feasible actuator travel.

The direct extensor route respects the wrap-release schedule of the route-elimination rule, releasing its six sequential contacts at knee angles of \ang{-89.8}, \ang{-55.7}, \ang{-47.8}, \ang{-20.2}, \ang{6.1}, and \ang{7.4}, and the optimized design places the femur-side origin at $(40.0, 137.0, 0)$~mm and the tibial insertion at $(34.4, -197.1, 0)$~mm, with a resting length of \qty{827.7}{\mm} and \qty{36.8}{\mm} of tendon.

The cam summaries use two different normalizations. For the evaluated knee-angle grid $\Theta$, let $M_h(\theta)$ be the human-reference torque, $M_{h,\max}$ its peak magnitude, and $M_{\mathbf{k}}(\theta)$ the predicted knee-axis torque. The minimum surplus above the required envelope and the minimum relative margin above the human reference are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
s_{\mathrm{req}} &= \min_{\theta\in\Theta}\frac{\lvert M_{\mathbf{k}}(\theta)\rvert-1.05\lvert M_h(\theta)\rvert}{M_{h,\max}},\\
m_h &= \min_{\theta\in\Theta}\frac{\lvert M_{\mathbf{k}}(\theta)\rvert-\lvert M_h(\theta)\rvert}{\lvert M_h(\theta)\rvert}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:cam_summary_definitions}
\end{equation}

The unadjusted cam optimizer solution had $s_{\mathrm{req}}=+31.30$~\% and $m_h=+42.34$~\%, using Equation~\eqref{eq:cam_summary_definitions}. The adjusted cam design is the one evaluated in the main paper. Its required pressure remains above the \qty{150}{\kPa} regulated floor used in the design calculation. The tibial wrap follows a bend radius of \qty{96.9}{\mm}, the guiding route point clears the femoral-condyle envelope by \qty{43.1}{\mm} at \ang{10} of knee extension, and the moment arm spans \qty{59.4}{\mm} to \qty{102.8}{\mm} with a mean of \qty{72.4}{\mm} (Supplementary Figure~\ref{fig:ExtensorRoute_redesigned}).

\subsection{Contact and Release Angles of the Wrapped Route}\label{app:contact_release}

The route-elimination rule that schedules the wrapped extensor route is a route-topology state machine rather than a shortest-path solver: the fully flexed route carries every candidate contact, and each contact is released at the first more-extended knee angle at which the straight chord between its surviving neighbors can replace it without entering the exclusion envelopes. This section states the calculation in generic terms, so that it applies to any wrapped actuator route on a rotating joint pair; the extensor route of Table~\ref{tab:RouteRedesign20} gives the cam-route example; its release schedule follows the same rule on the cam geometry.

The route is stored as a fixed number of rows $p_{1},\ldots,p_{9}$: rows $p_{1}$ through $p_{5}$ are femur-frame points, rows $p_{6}$ through $p_{9}$ are tibia-frame points, and the segment from $p_{5}$ to $p_{6}$ crosses from one body frame into the other. The endpoints $p_{1}$ and $p_{9}$ are the actuator origin and insertion, and they are always active. Each interior row is either active, a genuine wrap contact, or eliminated; an eliminated femur-side row repeats the preceding active femur row, and an eliminated tibia-side row repeats the neighboring active tibia row, so the route keeps a fixed size and its segment chain crosses the joint exactly once. Let $\mathcal{A}(\theta_{\mathbf{k}}) \subseteq \{2,\ldots,8\}$ denote the active interior rows at knee angle $\theta_{\mathbf{k}}$. The sweep over the design grid, here 100 poses from $\ang{-120}$ to $\ang{+10}$ at a spacing of \qty{1.31}{\degree}, proceeds from full flexion toward extension, each pose inherits the active set of the previous pose, and rows are only ever removed, so $\mathcal{A}$ shrinks monotonically as the knee extends.

At every pose, every active interior row is tested between its nearest active anchors,
\begin{equation}
i^{-}_{j} = \max\{i < j : i \in \mathcal{A} \cup \{1\}\}, \qquad
i^{+}_{j} = \min\{i > j : i \in \mathcal{A} \cup \{9\}\},
\label{eq:app_anchors}
\end{equation}
with the anchors expressed in the candidate's native frame through the per-angle joint transforms. Write $A$, $B$, and $C$ for the planar, sagittal positions of $p_{i^{-}_{j}}$, $p_{i^{+}_{j}}$, and $p_{j}$, and let $s(x, y)$ denote the signed angle from vector $x$ to vector $y$, positive counterclockwise, evaluated as a single $\operatorname{atan2}$ of the planar cross product and the dot product so that no principal-value seam can corrupt the comparison. The elimination margin of row $j$ is
\begin{equation}
\mu_{j}(\theta_{\mathbf{k}}) =
\begin{cases}
s\!\left(C - A,\; B - A\right), & j \leq 5,\\[4pt]
s\!\left(A - B,\; C - B\right), & j \geq 6,
\end{cases}
\label{eq:app_elim_margin}
\end{equation}
a signed angle between the through-contact leg and the bypass chord, measured in the native wrap sense of the candidate's side of the crossing segment; the two cases differ only in that sense of rotation, because the femur-side contacts turn the route one way around the distal femur and the tibia-side contacts turn it the opposite way around the proximal tibia. The rotation rule releases the contact once the margin turns positive, the orientation at which the chord, rather than the contact, provides the outer boundary of the route on that side. The gate is opened slightly early,
\begin{equation}
\mu_{j}(\theta_{\mathbf{k}}) > -\Delta, \qquad \Delta = \qty{1.5}{\degree},
\label{eq:app_rotation_gate}
\end{equation}
as a release hysteresis: in the last poses before the contact becomes collinear with its chord, the computed moment arm of the wrapping segment collapses, and releasing the contact slightly before that orientation, with the collision gate below still controlling validity, removes the artifact without changing the route family.

Opening the rotation gate is necessary but not sufficient. A removal additionally requires the straight bypass chord $\overline{AB}$ to clear the exclusion envelopes of the candidate's side,
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{dist}\!\left(\overline{AB}_{\mathrm{trim}},\, \mathcal{E}_{j}\right) \;\geq\; g, \qquad g = \qty{0.5}{\mm},
\label{eq:app_bypass_gate}
\end{equation}
where $\operatorname{dist}$ is the planar clearance between the trimmed chord and the envelope and tangency is allowed. The chord is trimmed by \qty{2}{\mm} at each end, capped at \qty{45}{\percent} of its length, because a legitimate contact departs from the clearance boundary itself. On the femur side the envelopes $\mathcal{E}_{j}$ are the femoral-cylinder clearance circle, the condyle clearance polygon, and the femoral-wall span; on the tibia side they are the two tibial-cylinder clearance circles and the wall band between them. Every envelope is the normal offset of a bone profile by the fully inflated BPA radius $R_{\mathrm{BPA}}$,
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{q}(\vartheta) = \mathbf{e}(\vartheta) + R_{\mathrm{BPA}}\,\hat{\mathbf{n}}(\vartheta),
\label{eq:app_offset_curve}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{e}(\vartheta)$ traces the physical profile and $\hat{\mathbf{n}}(\vartheta)$ is its outward normal; the offset curve shares the tangent of the physical profile, its radius of curvature is $\rho(\vartheta) = \rho_{\mathrm{phys}}(\vartheta) + R_{\mathrm{BPA}}$, and the candidate contact positions themselves are seeded at tangent points of this curve. Because the envelopes are drawn at the fully inflated radius while the routed BPA is contracted at the release angles, each envelope radius may be relaxed by $\rho = \qty{3}{\mm}$ inside the gate, floored at the bone profile plus \qty{0.5}{\mm}; a rotation-gate opening that still fails the relaxed collision gate indicates that the seed placement, not the gate, must move.

At the first pose of the sweep, full flexion, the seed route is settled to a fixpoint: every contact whose two gates already open there is removed, and removals repeat until no further gate opens, so that the sweep starts from a valid topology; a colinearity guard may additionally remove a seed contact that starts the sweep already aligned with its neighbors, and its tolerance is zero in the design of record, so every seed row of Table~\ref{tab:RouteRedesign20} starts active. At every later pose at most one contact is released: if several rows open both gates in the same pose, the row with the largest elimination margin, the one whose rotation rule is most decisively open, is released first, and the remaining rows are retested at the next pose. The release angle of row $j$ is recorded as the first grid pose at which the row is inactive. The activation angle is read from the same record with the sweep reversed, as the last pose at which the row is active, which is the pose at which the contact re-engages when the route is driven from extension toward flexion.

The same active-set record drives the geometric bend measure that scales the fictive-strain wrapping loss of Equations~\eqref{eq:deltaL} and~\eqref{eq:x3} inside the stiffness-aware force balance. Each wrap family, the femoral-cylinder contact, the femoral-condyle curve, the tibial contact curve, and the crossing-point curve at the ICR, contributes an arc length over its effective radius, and the total
\begin{equation}
b(\theta_{\mathbf{k}}) = \sum_{f} R_{f}(\theta_{\mathbf{k}})\, \alpha_{f}(\theta_{\mathbf{k}})
\label{eq:app_bend_measure}
\end{equation}
is the bend signal handed to the force model. Each family's turn angle $\alpha_{f}$ ramps with knee angle between the two route-state events that bound it, holding zero before the first event and its plateau after the second, so the topology state and the bending signal are two readings of one record. The formulation is joint-agnostic: it requires only a fixed route representation with one crossing segment, per-angle transforms between the two body frames, and clearance envelopes on each side, so it carries unchanged to the other wrapped routes of this program.


\subsection{Transmission Sizing and Configuration Selection}\label{app:transmission_selection}

The transmission search retained each site's attachment routing while varying the number of parallel BPAs $N_B$, the travel multiplier $G$, resting length, and tendon length. For the schematic in the main paper, $G=n$, the number of cable parts supporting the moving sheave. The ideal force gain relative to one BPA is $N_B/G$; for example, three BPAs through a two-part tackle deliver 1.5 times the force and twice the travel of one directly attached BPA. Lengths were re-solved for each visited transmission configuration.

Let $N_D$ be the number of included joint-coordinate cells after applying the hip-domain mask. The resting and tendon lengths were selected by minimizing
\begin{equation}
J_{\mathrm{survey}}=-10^3 m-a_{\mathrm{search}}+10^{-3}d,
\label{eq:survey_selection_cost}
\end{equation}
with the dimensionless terms defined in Table~\ref{tab:survey_selection_terms}. Invalid model evaluations received a cost of $10^6$. Slack and infeasible cells otherwise contributed through these soft terms; the length bounds were enforced directly. The search's positive-strain tie-break is distinct from the positive-insertion-tension coverage reported in the main paper, which evaluates the saved configurations.

\begin{table}[htbp]
\centering
\begingroup\fontsize{10pt}{12pt}\selectfont
\caption{Terms in the transmission-sizing objective. All counts use the included grid cells.}
\label{tab:survey_selection_terms}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}lXr@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Term} & \textbf{Definition} & \textbf{Coefficient} \\
\midrule
$m$ & Fraction of cells with taut, feasible BPA torque matching the human torque's sign and meeting its magnitude. & $-10^3$ \\
$a_{\mathrm{search}}$ & Fraction of taut, feasible cells with finite positive model strain and nonzero torque, as used during the search. & $-1$ \\
$d$ & Sum of the slack-cell and infeasible-cell counts, divided by $N_D$. & $10^{-3}$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\endgroup
\end{table}

For a configuration, pattern search started from the lowest-cost of five box seeds. If $L_{\mathrm{char}}$ is the maximum rigid route length over the evaluated grid, the resting-length bounds were $[0.30L_{\mathrm{char}},0.90L_{\mathrm{char}}]$ and the tendon-length bounds were $[0.02,\max(0.30L_{\mathrm{char}},0.06)]$~m. The mesh and step tolerances were $10^{-4}$.

Each site started at $(N_B,G)=(1,1)$. A torque shortfall among taut, feasible cells relative to the peak human torque magnitude prompted an additional parallel BPA; slack or infeasible cells prompted an additional cable part. Both counts were capped at three, with at most five visited configurations, 300 evaluations, and approximately ten minutes per site. Among the visited configurations, the saved winner maximized the integer number of cells meeting the human torque requirement, then the search-active cell count, then preferred smaller $N_B+G$. A remaining tie retained the earlier configuration. These rules select among the evaluated designs rather than establish a global optimum.

\subsection{Knee coordinate transformations}\label{sec:knee_frames}
The routing tables specify points on two moving bodies. Let $F$ denote the femur frame, $T$ the tibia-fixed frame at $\theta_1$, and $K$ the frame at the instantaneous center of rotation (ICR), with axes parallel to $T$. The homogeneous transform $\mathbf T_{AB}$ maps a point expressed in $B$ into $A$. For a tibial attachment $\mathbf q_T$, write $\bar{\mathbf q}_T=[\mathbf q_T^{\mathsf T},1]^{\mathsf T}$. At knee angle $\theta_{\mathbf k}$,
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf T_{FK}&=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf R_z(\theta_{\mathbf k}) & \mathbf c_F\\\mathbf 0^{\mathsf T}&1\end{bmatrix},&
\mathbf T_{KT}&=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf I & -\mathbf c_T\\\mathbf 0^{\mathsf T}&1\end{bmatrix},&
\bar{\mathbf q}_F&=\mathbf T_{FK}\mathbf T_{KT}\bar{\mathbf q}_T .
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
Here $\mathbf c_F$ and $\mathbf c_T$ locate the same ICR in the femur and tibia frames. Subtracting $\mathbf c_T$ first expresses the attachment relative to the ICR. The rotation and translation then place it in the femur frame, where lengths between successive route points can be calculated. Both translations have zero $z$ component. The four in-plane components are interpolated separately by cubic splines through Table~\ref{tab:knee_frame_knots}. For example, at zero knee angle the tibial origin is at $(21.09-27.40,\,-417.28-25.53,\,0)=(-6.31,\,-442.81,\,0)$ mm in the femur frame.

For a measured load, let $L$ be the reaction-force frame and write its wrench as $\mathcal F_L=[\mathbf m_L^{\mathsf T},\mathbf f_L^{\mathsf T}]^{\mathsf T}$, with moment first. The measured knee torque is the $z$ component of the moment in $\mathcal F_K=\operatorname{Ad}_{\mathbf T_{LK}}^{\mathsf T}\mathcal F_L$, where $\mathbf T_{LK}=\mathbf T_{LT}\mathbf T_{TK}$. This transformation carries the measured force and its moment arm to the moving knee center. Coordinates in the tables are converted to meters when computing torque in newton meters.
