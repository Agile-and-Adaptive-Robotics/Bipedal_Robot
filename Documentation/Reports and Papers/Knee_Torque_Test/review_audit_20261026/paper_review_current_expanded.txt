Deleted: The redesign results carry caveats that bound their use. The flexor redesign solves its second actuator's endpoints from the first by the mirrored-endpoint rule rather than optimizing the two routes independently, so the mirror symmetry is built into the design; freeing the second route's endpoints is the next design refinement. All redesign solutions are optimizer-stage: they have not been propagated into the simulation models, and the physical validation rests on sparse isometric holds, six points on the extensor cam route and fifteen on the flexor route, rather than dense sweeps of either configuration. The stiffness pair itself was selected by an external design requirement rather than uniquely identified, because the pinned-flexor data admit a family of near-equivalent pairs; an independent stiffness measurement of the test stands would close that non-identifiability and either confirm or replace the adopted pair. The wrapping factor $\chi_{3}$ was identified on the $\diameter$\qty{10}{\mm} extensor family and applied unchanged to the $\diameter$\qty{20}{\mm} redesigns, so its transfer across actuator diameters is untested. On the characterization side, the fictive-strain formulation inherits the conclusion of our force characterization that the unloaded \qty{620}{\kPa} contraction $\varepsilon_{620}$ should be recorded for each actuator used on a robot \citep{bolen_2026}, since every correction term is normalized by it. The whole-body extension identified its own next steps: the route-builder assembly failure needs a fix before the twelve terminated routes can be rebuilt, and the two torque-short feasible routes need either larger actuators or relaxed corridors.
