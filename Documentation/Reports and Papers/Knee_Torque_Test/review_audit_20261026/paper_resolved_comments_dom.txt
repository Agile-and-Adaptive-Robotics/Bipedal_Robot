In the cam variant, the tibial-side route points trace a cam profile, so the routing geometry itself is shaped rather than only its endpoints. The pulley variants make the transmission ratio itself a design variable: a reeved tackle divides the force delivered at the insertion by its number of moving line parts $G$ and multiplies the insertion travel per unit of actuator contraction by the same factor, so the tackle converts surplus actuator force into range of motion, and the surplus is supplied by mounting BPAs in parallel or mirrored pairs, the same two-actuator-per-route arrangement the knee designs of this paper already use. With $n$ actuators in parallel feeding a tackle of $G$ line parts, the transmission's force gain is $n/G$ and its travel gain is $G$; for example, three actuators through a two-part tackle deliver 1.5 times the force of a single actuator at twice the travel. The mono-pulley variant reeves the tendon over a single tackle and serves uniarticular muscles, which cross one joint, and the bi-pulley variant reeves one tackle per joint crossed and serves biarticular and multi-articular muscles; the bi-pulley machinery was applied as a batch over five anatomical muscle sites of the robot-body model's leg.
