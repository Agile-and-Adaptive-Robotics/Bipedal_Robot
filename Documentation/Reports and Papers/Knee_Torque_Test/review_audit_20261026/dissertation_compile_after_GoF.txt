    - generic: "current match. The goodness-of-fit metrics used to judge the optimization results are the root mean square error (RMSE), the fraction of variance unexplained (FVU), and the maximum absolute residual, each computed between the measured and predicted isometric torque over the tested range of motion. Each metric isolates a different aspect of disagreement between the two curves. RMSE reports the overall magnitude of the error and, when the curve shapes already agree, is dominated by the remaining level offset. FVU normalizes the error variance by the variance of the measured torque, making it scale-free: it asks whether the prediction reproduces the variation of the measurement rather than whether it is close in absolute units, is the complement of the Nash--Sutcliffe model efficiency \\citep{nash_river_1970}, and takes values below one when the prediction outperforms the mean of the measurement itself. The maximum absolute residual is a worst-case local measure. A small shift of the predicted curve along the knee-angle axis produces large pointwise residuals where the torque gradient is steep and small residuals elsewhere, so this metric is sensitive to phase misalignment while responding only weakly to uniform level offsets. This interpretation follows the standard decomposition of curve-comparison error into offset, amplitude, and pattern components \\citep{taylor_summarizing_2001}: the mean square error between two curves separates into the squared mean offset, an amplitude mismatch, and a residual shape term. An equivalent three-term formulation, splitting waveform similarity into offset, amplitude, and shape, underlies the linear fit method developed for clinical gait waveform comparison \\citep{iosa_assessment_2014}. The metrics used here map onto this decomposition, with RMSE carrying the level disagreement, FVU the shape and amplitude agreement, and the maximum absolute residual serving as the phase-sensitive worst case. FVU is treated as the primary criterion because it is the only one of the three that is directly comparable across tests with different torque scales. \\subsection{Optimization on line 310."
