Alt text for spiking_mirror_ground_gait.pdf

Four panels of joint-angle traces over a 16-second ground walk of the
converted MuJoCo robot, comparing the non-spiking spinal-network winner
(left column) with the untuned spiking-neuron mirror run at the same winner
parameters (right column). Top row: knee angle in degrees. In panel A
(non-spiking), the dashed orange right-leg trace sweeps repeatedly between
about minus 101 and plus 8 degrees for nine cycles, while the solid indigo
left-leg trace moves through a single slow excursion between about minus 42
and plus 15 degrees without cycling: only the right leg steps. In panel B
(spiking mirror), the solid indigo left trace and the dashed orange right
trace both cycle with deep flexion, the right leg completing eight cycles
between about minus 86 and plus 13 degrees and the left leg nine cycles
between about minus 85 and minus 8 degrees, with a cycle knee minimum of
minus 79.8 degrees. Bottom row: hip flexion angle in degrees. In panel C
(non-spiking), the right hip oscillates between about minus 18 and plus 44
degrees while the left hip rocks between about minus 21 and plus 2 degrees.
In panel D (spiking mirror), the right hip oscillates between about minus
35 and plus 43 degrees and the left hip between about minus 35 and plus 13
degrees. The figure shows that the untuned spiking mirror, although it
scores worse overall, produces a bilateral gait that the non-spiking
winner cannot.
