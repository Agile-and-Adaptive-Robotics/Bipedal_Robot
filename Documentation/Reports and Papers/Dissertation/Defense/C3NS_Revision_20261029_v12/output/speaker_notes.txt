01 [core] Movement and Control of Biomimetic Humanoid Robots
My dissertation develops ways to predict actuator force, compare robot joints with human torque targets, and test sensory control. The research robot serves as an experimental platform for studying how mechanics and neural control work together.

02 [core] Robots on warehouse floors face different challenges from robots on rubble
Wheels work efficiently on prepared warehouse floors. Rubble introduces changing contacts and disturbances. These photographs motivate our interest in compliant legs and neural control; they are not a controlled comparison of the two robots.

03 [core] Biomimetic robots let us test mechanics and neural control
Biology informs robot design, while biomimetic robots let us intervene in mechanics and neural pathways in ways that would be impossible in humans. Better prosthetic and orthotic devices are a later application. Kengoro illustrates physical biomimicry; the AARL leg is our research platform.

04 [core] Four knowledge and tool gaps for biomimetic robot research
Four questions organize the dissertation: what force an actuator can produce, how a real joint transmits it, where to find sensory evidence with its context, and how to test candidate neural mechanisms. SADb addresses the evidence-access question.

28 [question] Joint torque depends on force direction and moment arm
Torque follows the cross product of the attachment position and tensile force. Its magnitude is force times perpendicular moment arm. Changing a route also changes actuator excursion, so moment arm alone is insufficient.
