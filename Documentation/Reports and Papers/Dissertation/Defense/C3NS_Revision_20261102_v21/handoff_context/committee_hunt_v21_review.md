## Narrative assessment

**Opening, 1–11.** The sequence establishes uneven-terrain motivation, BPA compliance, force and moment arm, sensory interaction, and the anatomical comparison before the limited-range and torque problems. The reciprocal biology–robot loop leads to three research gaps. Background precedes the goals, and SADb is absent from the main sequence. The historical range plot on 7 is scoped in notes as a model comparison limited by both contraction and mechanical interference; preserve that oral qualification rather than presenting it as an isolated measurement of contraction's effect.

**Force, 12–28.** Definitions and existing-model failures motivate apparatus and protocol on 17–18, followed by the new results. The early failures are problem evidence, not an out-of-order claim about the new experiment. The requested right-side equations on 19–20, the pressure-limit interpretation on 21, fixed 620 kPa normalization definitions on 23, and generalized equation and coefficients on 24 are readable. The two complete native goodness-of-fit tables on 27–28 now close this section before the torque divider on 29. No table values are lost to the two-slide split.

The existing-model-only panel on 16 still has measured force on the horizontal axis; only proposed-model prediction points are absent. Its precise `l₀ = 450 mm, ⌀20 mm BPA` caption prevents the broad title from implying that this single panel contains both diameters. The preceding 10 mm example and the later four-case tables supply the broader context. Source equations, coefficients, all 36 table values, and saved-figure conservation were checked during force authoring against the frozen submitted chapter and final-proof paper; this assembled check confirms layout, not a second independent numerical audit.

**Mechanics, 29–56.** Selected human-muscle targets precede the apparatus, mismatch, and measured-length evidence. Local frames, correction terms, the coupled force/displacement solve, identification, and fixed-parameter validation then precede route redesign. GIF frames and route geometry appear before redesigned torque results. The cam comparison distinguishes force at 620 kPa from pressure for the same target, avoiding an unsupported universal pressure-reduction claim. The pulley schematic precedes the whole-leg prediction. Notes on 41 explicitly state that validation informed Pareto acceptance and that stiffness represents effective system compliance, not independently identified bracket material properties. Keep these qualifications in the spoken explanation.

The whole-leg result on 56 is labelled predicted, identifies 27 sites and 620 kPa, and names actuator sizing and pulley ratios. Its notes define the numbers as equal-site means of trapezoid-weighted angular-domain coverage. It does not claim an attachment-only intervention or whole-leg physical validation. The historical BFLH illustrations on 8/33 and selected monoarticular BFSH/VM targets on 32 should remain verbally distinct.

**Neural tools and demonstrations, 57–71.** The failed hand-tuning/transfer example motivates repeatable testing. The Table 6.1 progression then introduces model bodies, blocks, CAD mechanics, the interface, staged experiments, and circuit tests before scoped runtime and body results. The 6.82× complete-process and 18.31× neural-call figures on 67 match the saved paired benchmark report; the notes correctly separate that benchmark from the final body trial and avoid a universal real-time claim.

The RF/HAM diagram on 68 and recorded activity on 69 form a useful structural-to-functional pair, after the clipping is repaired. Explain that the knee motor-neuron traces on 69 are representative VI/BFSH branches, not the direct RF/HAM motor outputs drawn on 68. The figure contains no explicit misleading arrows, so a spoken distinction is sufficient. Similarly, PF/DF on 71 means plantarflexion/dorsiflexion; PF elsewhere denotes pattern formation. The notes already make this distinction.

The 48.8% BW assistance and 115.6 N horizontal guidance on 70 match `SUPPORT_DECOMPOSITION_5_TO_13.json` (net mean vertical assistance and horizontal-restraint RMS, respectively). The qualifying-swing counts refer to 5–13 s, while the replay and displayed traces cover a shorter window; notes disclose this. The quarter/original contact-strength comparison on 71 matches the source caption's 5/4 versus 2/2 qualifying swings. It supports a configuration-specific feedback conclusion, not unsupported walking or a generally optimal gain.

**Contributions and future work, 72–77.** Physical force/torque evidence is recapped separately from dynamic testing. Support reduction, phase-specific contact testing, and hardware comparison are framed as proposed work. The closing three contributions are concrete and preserve the selected-target and supported-model limits. The proposed phase-gated controller is not presented as already implemented or tested.
