# Teaching-animation repairs

Use `build_fixed_20261105/` for the corrected GIFs and stills. The original
`build/` renders and the earlier README's cam-registration explanation are
superseded. The earlier inferred cam placement and Assem2.75 alignment are
not used in the corrected cam animation.

## Cam: authoritative CAD

Ben identified `Knee assembly/09_BA_003.SLDASM` as the assembly of record.
The corrected renderer uses its native component tessellations, resolved
component transforms, link-hole centers, and named **Theta1 Coordinate
System**. These read-only extractions are in `data/09_BA_003/`.

The cam remains in its assembly placement relative to the tibia. The
linkage closes geometrically at each angle, and the pivot marker and its
leader follow the intersection of the two links. Tibia-side route points
are transformed from t1, as in `Knee_Flexor_Data_20mm.m`; femur-side points
retain the saved design's femur coordinates. The translucent local BPA
envelope uses the saved design radius, 19.25 mm. The thin blue path is its
centerline, not its outside surface.

No optimizer result, manuscript, experimental measurement, or torque plot
was changed. This is a CAD kinematic teaching animation. Its display
moment arm is drawn from the actual CAD link intersection.

Native tessellations avoid STL export's positive-coordinate translation.
The part-origin/frame assumptions in the discarded Assem2.75 attempt are
not carried into this renderer.

## Reverse pulley

The repaired schematic preserves cable length, including the changing
tangent at the fixed redirect. The output tendon pulls the tibia, and the
crossed linkage closes as the tibia moves. The marker follows the link
intersection. Output cable travel is twice BPA shortening, with half the
combined BPA force in the ideal relation. This is an illustrative
mechanism based on the source figure, not a dynamic or efficiency result.

## Gait2392 frames

Both shaft and arrowhead move with the tibia. Axis labels remain upright,
the camera contains the parent axes, and the GIF is actually assembled.
Parent and child origins remain those of the source anatomical model.

## Rebuild

The fourth visual is the matched human/BPA knee-range comparison on slide 19.
Both views use the same Gait2392 anatomy, with the camera on OpenSim +Z
and +Y up. The hip stays at zero. The human view spans the full plotted
interval, −120° to +8.7°; the right view clamps that same motion to the
saved curve's nonzero-BPA interval, −38.1° to +8.7°. It pauses at the limit
while the human view continues. Moving dots link the poses to the unchanged
curves. The last zero sample is −39.4°; the limit is taken conservatively
from the first nonzero sample. Ben explicitly waived bodyweight.

The graph arrays are in `data/human_robot_rom/torque_source.csv`.
Run `scripts/build_visual4_rom.py` in the machine's existing MuJoCo/myo
Python environment. This renderer prescribes coordinates in the
MyoConverter copy of Gait2392, applies its coupled-joint translations,
and verifies the camera against the original OpenSim axes. It does not
integrate dynamics. Output is `build_fixed_20261105/visual4/`, including
separate model GIFs and one synchronized composite for PowerPoint.

Run `scripts/build_visual1_cam.py`, `build_visual2_pulley.py`, and
`build_visual3_frames.py` with Blender, then `scripts/make_gifs.py` with
Python/Pillow. Add `-- --stills-only` to a Blender call for labeled and
unlabeled stills. Source geometry is already extracted; regenerating the
renders does not require rerunning MATLAB, SOLIDWORKS, or any experiment.

`build_fixed_20261105/GIF_MANIFEST.json` records encoded files and hashes.
Original renders remain available for comparison in `build/`.
