\chapter{Appendix C: Model Geometry, Bracket Frames, and Compliance Parameters}\label{app:model_geometry}
\graphicspath{{../Figures/94-AppendixC/}{figs/94-AppendixC/}}

This appendix documents the numeric geometry and compliance parameters behind the force and torque models of Chapters~\ref{ch:muscles} and~\ref{ch:torque} as they exist in the project's MATLAB code, with file names given so that any value can be traced to the code that produced it. Route and bracket coordinates are coded in meters and reported here in millimeters; stiffnesses are coded and reported in newtons per meter. The bracket reference points are interpretation choices for the 3D-printed (Onyx FDM) attachment hardware, set from CAD by me; moving a reference point changes the identified stiffness values, so the points are recorded here alongside the values they produced.

\section{Static BPA Pathway Data}

The static BPA model routes each actuator as a straight-segment polyline through via points stored in a per-test \texttt{Location} array of dimensions $M\times3\times N$, where $N$ is the number of joint frames in the motion sweep and $M$ the number of via points; the first row in the distal link frame is marked by a cross-point index. There are no binormal or surface-frame quantities: at each frame the force direction is the normalized vector from the cross-point row to the previous row transformed into the crossing frame,
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{d}=\mathbf{R}_{ft}^{\mathsf T}(\mathbf{p}_{C-1}-\mathbf{t}_{ft})-\mathbf{p}_{C},\qquad \hat{\mathbf{u}}=\frac{\mathbf{d}}{\lVert\mathbf{d}\rVert},
    \label{eq:app_forcedir}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{p}_{C}$ is expressed in the tibia frame, $\mathbf{p}_{C-1}$ in the femur frame, and $\mathbf{T}_{ft}=[\mathbf{R}_{ft},\mathbf{t}_{ft};\mathbf{0}^{\mathsf T},1]$ maps tibia coordinates to femur coordinates. The inverse transform includes both rotation and translation, as in the implemented point transformation [Provenance: \nolinkurl{minimizeFlxPin.m} lines 160 and 524--537 (\texttt{UD}) and 201--235 (\texttt{computeForceVector}); \nolinkurl{minimizeExtX3.m} lines 687--700; BPA struct fields, \nolinkurl{minimizeFlxPin.m} lines 28--35]. The pinned-knee test routes are archived as \nolinkurl{FlxPinBPASet.mat} (5 flexor tests, $M=2$, knee angle $-120^{\circ}$ to $+10^{\circ}$) and \nolinkurl{ExtPinBPASet.mat} (9 extensor tests, $M=7$, knee angle $-125^{\circ}$ to $+35^{\circ}$).

\section{Joint Transformation Matrices}

The knee transform is planar: every joint frame is a rotation about $z$ by the knee angle $\theta_{\mathbf{k}}$ composed with a translation fitted to robot CAD tables of the migrating instantaneous center of rotation. With $\mathbf{R} = \mathbf{R}_{z}(\theta_{\mathbf{k}})$, the robot (femur$\to$ICR) transform is $\mathbf{T}_{\text{Pam}} = [\mathbf{R},\; [f_{3}(\theta_{\mathbf{k}}),\,f_{4}(\theta_{\mathbf{k}}),\,0]^{\top};\; \mathbf{0}^{\top}\;1]$ with $f_{3}, f_{4}$ cubic-spline fits (ICR $x$: 23.30 to 10.47~mm; $y$: $-416.65$ to $-395.66$~mm over $\theta_{\mathbf{k}} = 0.17$ to $-2.62$~rad), and the ICR$\to$tibia transform is the pure translation $[f_{13}(\theta_{\mathbf{k}}),\,f_{14}(\theta_{\mathbf{k}}),\,0]^{\top}$ ($x$: 29.66 to $-7.58$~mm; $y$: 25.97 to 22.33~mm), composed as $\mathbf{T}_{t1\to f} = \mathbf{T}_{t1\to\text{ICR}}\;\mathbf{T}_{\text{Pam}}^{-1}$ over 100 frames spanning $\theta_{\mathbf{k}} \in [-120^{\circ}, +10^{\circ}]$ [Provenance: \nolinkurl{buildKneeFlexorContext20mm.m} lines 34--80; \nolinkurl{buildKneeExtContext20mm.m} lines 30--78; \nolinkurl{Functions/ModernRobotics/RpToTrans.m}]. In the archived test sets the per-frame transform maps tibia to femur/ICR and takes the form
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{T}_{\mathbf{k}}(\theta_{\mathbf{k}}) \;=\;
    \begin{bmatrix}
    \cos\theta_{\mathbf{k}} & -\sin\theta_{\mathbf{k}} & 0 & d_{x}(\theta_{\mathbf{k}})\\
    \sin\theta_{\mathbf{k}} & \cos\theta_{\mathbf{k}} & 0 & d_{y}(\theta_{\mathbf{k}})\\
    0 & 0 & 1 & 0\\
    0 & 0 & 0 & 1
    \end{bmatrix},
    \label{eq:app_Tk}
\end{equation}
with rotation block $\mathbf{R}_{z}(\theta_{\mathbf{k}})$ and translation components $d_{x}, d_{y}$ in meters. In the archived flexor test set the translation is constant across the sweep, $[d_{x},\,d_{y},\,0] = [4.5,\,-395.8,\,0]$~mm, and the rotation sweeps from $\mathbf{R}_{z}(-120^{\circ})$ at full flexion to $\mathbf{R}_{z}(+10^{\circ})$ at full extension (\nolinkurl{FlxPinBPASet.mat}, kf(1)).

\section{Bracket Reference Points and Frames}

Table~\ref{tab:app_bracket_points} lists the bracket reference points. Each compliant bracket carries an instantaneous body-fixed frame anchored at its reference point, constructed by two rotations (yaw about $z$, then pitch about the yawed $y$). With $\mathbf{p}_{b}$ the vector from the reference point to the bracket-to-BPA attachment target, the yaw and pitch angles are
\begin{equation}
    \theta_{br} = \operatorname{atan2}(p_{by},\, p_{bx}),
    \qquad
    \phi_{br} = \operatorname{atan2}(p'_{bz},\, p'_{bx})
    \quad\text{with}\quad
    \mathbf{p}'_{b} = \mathbf{R}_{z}(\theta_{br})^{\top}\mathbf{p}_{b},
    \label{eq:app_bracketangles}
\end{equation}
and the bracket-frame pose is
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{R}_{kbr} = \mathbf{R}_{z}(\theta_{br})\,\mathbf{R}_{y}(\phi_{br})^{\top},
    \qquad
    \mathbf{T}_{kbr} = \left[\mathbf{R}_{kbr},\; \mathbf{p}_{br};\; \mathbf{0}\;\,1\right],
    \label{eq:app_bracketframe}
\end{equation}
The frame's $x$ axis follows the nominal load path, with angles calculated from the bracket geometry. Both brackets in the pinned-flexor model and the pinned extensor use the two-rotation construction of Equation~\eqref{eq:app_bracketframe}. Forces are transformed into each bracket frame, and displaced attachment points are transformed back into the limb frame. The symmetric insertion stiffness array is invariant to the additional rotation; the origin array is not (Table~\ref{tab:app_stiffness_arrays}).

\section{Stiffness Arrays and Compliance}

Every configuration models bracket compliance as a diagonal Hooke-law spring in the bracket frame, $\mathbf{K}_{br} = \operatorname{diag}(k_{1}, k_{2}, k_{3})$ newtons per meter. The force-path projection and series combination of the compliances are defined in Chapter~\ref{ch:torque}, Equations~\eqref{eq:bktstiffness}--\eqref{eq:keff}.

When an artificial tendon is present, its axial spring ($\times2$ cables, effective steel area $1.51\times10^{-6}$~m$^2$, $E=193$~GPa) adds in series. The force balance of Equation~\eqref{eq:complianceforcebalance} is solved at each pose by \texttt{fzero} on the contraction interval. Table~\ref{tab:app_stiffness_arrays} gives the configuration-specific stiffness arrays.

Table~\ref{tab:app_stiffness_arrays} assigns the fitted variables $X_1$ and $X_2$ to the bracket-frame axes, in N/m. \section{Contact and Release Angles of the Wrapped Route}\label{app:contact_release}

The route-elimination rule that schedules the wrapped extensor route is a route-topology state machine rather than a shortest-path solver: the fully flexed route carries every candidate contact, and each contact is released at the first more-extended knee angle at which the straight chord between its surviving neighbors can replace it without entering the exclusion envelopes. This section states the calculation in generic terms, so that it applies to any wrapped actuator route on a rotating joint pair; the extensor route of Table~\ref{tab:app_ext_seed} is the worked example, and the release schedule the calculation produces is the one quoted there.

The route is stored as a fixed number of rows $p_{1},\ldots,p_{9}$: rows $p_{1}$ through $p_{5}$ are femur-frame points, rows $p_{6}$ through $p_{9}$ are tibia-frame points, and the segment from $p_{5}$ to $p_{6}$ crosses from one body frame into the other. The endpoints $p_{1}$ and $p_{9}$ are the actuator origin and insertion, and they are always active. Each interior row is either active, a genuine wrap contact, or eliminated; an eliminated femur-side row repeats the preceding active femur row, and an eliminated tibia-side row repeats the neighboring active tibia row, so the route keeps a fixed size and its segment chain crosses the joint exactly once. Let $\mathcal{A}(\theta_{\mathbf{k}}) \subseteq \{2,\ldots,8\}$ denote the active interior rows at knee angle $\theta_{\mathbf{k}}$. The sweep over the design grid, here 100 poses from $\ang{-120}$ to $\ang{+10}$ at a spacing of \qty{1.31}{\degree}, proceeds from full flexion toward extension, each pose inherits the active set of the previous pose, and rows are only ever removed, so $\mathcal{A}$ shrinks monotonically as the knee extends.

At every pose, every active interior row is tested between its nearest active anchors,
\begin{equation}
i^{-}_{j} = \max\{i < j : i \in \mathcal{A} \cup \{1\}\}, \qquad
i^{+}_{j} = \min\{i > j : i \in \mathcal{A} \cup \{9\}\},
\label{eq:app_anchors}
\end{equation}
with the anchors expressed in the candidate's native frame through the per-angle joint transforms. Write $A$, $B$, and $C$ for the planar, sagittal positions of $p_{i^{-}_{j}}$, $p_{i^{+}_{j}}$, and $p_{j}$, and let $s(x, y)$ denote the signed angle from vector $x$ to vector $y$, positive counterclockwise, evaluated as a single $\operatorname{atan2}$ of the planar cross product and the dot product so that no principal-value seam can corrupt the comparison. The elimination margin of row $j$ is
\begin{equation}
\mu_{j}(\theta_{\mathbf{k}}) =
\begin{cases}
s\!\left(C - A,\; B - A\right), & j \leq 5,\\[4pt]
s\!\left(A - B,\; C - B\right), & j \geq 6,
\end{cases}
\label{eq:app_elim_margin}
\end{equation}
a signed angle between the through-contact leg and the bypass chord, measured in the native wrap sense of the candidate's side of the crossing segment; the two cases differ only in that sense of rotation, because the femur-side contacts turn the route one way around the distal femur and the tibia-side contacts turn it the opposite way around the proximal tibia. The rotation rule releases the contact once the margin turns positive, the orientation at which the chord, rather than the contact, provides the outer boundary of the route on that side. The gate is opened slightly early,
\begin{equation}
\mu_{j}(\theta_{\mathbf{k}}) > -\Delta, \qquad \Delta = \qty{1.5}{\degree},
\label{eq:app_rotation_gate}
\end{equation}
as a release hysteresis: in the last poses before the contact becomes collinear with its chord, the computed moment arm of the wrapping segment collapses, and releasing the contact slightly before that orientation, with the collision gate below still controlling validity, removes the artifact without changing the route family.

Opening the rotation gate is necessary but not sufficient. A removal additionally requires the straight bypass chord $\overline{AB}$ to clear the exclusion envelopes of the candidate's side,
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{dist}\!\left(\overline{AB}_{\mathrm{trim}},\, \mathcal{E}_{j}\right) \;\geq\; g, \qquad g = \qty{0.5}{\mm},
\label{eq:app_bypass_gate}
\end{equation}
where $\operatorname{dist}$ is the planar clearance between the trimmed chord and the envelope and tangency is allowed. The chord is trimmed by \qty{2}{\mm} at each end, capped at \qty{45}{\percent} of its length, because a legitimate contact departs from the clearance boundary itself. On the femur side the envelopes $\mathcal{E}_{j}$ are the femoral-cylinder clearance circle, the condyle clearance polygon, and the femoral-wall span; on the tibia side they are the two tibial-cylinder clearance circles and the wall band between them. Every envelope is the normal offset of a bone profile by the fully inflated BPA radius $R_{\mathrm{BPA}}$,
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{q}(\vartheta) = \mathbf{e}(\vartheta) + R_{\mathrm{BPA}}\,\hat{\mathbf{n}}(\vartheta),
\label{eq:app_offset_curve}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{e}(\vartheta)$ traces the physical profile and $\hat{\mathbf{n}}(\vartheta)$ is its outward normal; the offset curve shares the tangent of the physical profile, its radius of curvature is $\rho(\vartheta) = \rho_{\mathrm{phys}}(\vartheta) + R_{\mathrm{BPA}}$, and the candidate contact positions themselves are seeded at tangent points of this curve. Because the envelopes are drawn at the fully inflated radius while the routed BPA is contracted at the release angles, each envelope radius may be relaxed by $\rho = \qty{3}{\mm}$ inside the gate, floored at the bone profile plus \qty{0.5}{\mm}; a rotation-gate opening that still fails the relaxed collision gate indicates that the seed placement, not the gate, must move.

At the first pose of the sweep, full flexion, the seed route is settled to a fixpoint: every contact whose two gates already open there is removed, and removals repeat until no further gate opens, so that the sweep starts from a valid topology; a colinearity guard may additionally remove a seed contact that starts the sweep already aligned with its neighbors, and its tolerance is zero in the design of record, so every seed row of Table~\ref{tab:app_ext_seed} starts active. At every later pose at most one contact is released: if several rows open both gates in the same pose, the row with the largest elimination margin, the one whose rotation rule is most decisively open, is released first, and the remaining rows are retested at the next pose. The release angle of row $j$ is recorded as the first grid pose at which the row is inactive. The activation angle is read from the same record with the sweep reversed, as the last pose at which the row is active, which is the pose at which the contact re-engages when the route is driven from extension toward flexion.

The same active-set record drives the geometric bend measure that scales the fictive-strain wrapping loss of Equations~\eqref{eq:deltaL} and~\eqref{eq:x3} inside the stiffness-aware force balance. Each wrap family, the femoral-cylinder contact, the femoral-condyle curve, the tibial contact curve, and the crossing-point curve at the ICR, contributes an arc length over its effective radius, and the total
\begin{equation}
b(\theta_{\mathbf{k}}) = \sum_{f} R_{f}(\theta_{\mathbf{k}})\, \alpha_{f}(\theta_{\mathbf{k}})
\label{eq:app_bend_measure}
\end{equation}
is the bend signal handed to the force model. Each family's turn angle $\alpha_{f}$ ramps with knee angle between the two route-state events that bound it, holding zero before the first event and its plateau after the second, so the topology state and the bending signal are two readings of one record. The formulation is joint-agnostic: it requires only a fixed route representation with one crossing segment, per-angle transforms between the two body frames, and clearance envelopes on each side, so it carries unchanged to the other wrapped routes of this program.

\section{Supporting Route Searches and Transmission Sweeps}\label{sec:app_routing_extensions}

The transmission studies extend the route search by allowing an artificial tendon to pass through a tackle before insertion. A redirect changes the force direction; a multi-part tackle also changes the relation between actuator travel and insertion force. The comparisons below re-solve actuator sizes for each transmission configuration and evaluate insertion-side torque against the same human reference. They remain model predictions; physical tests of the redesigned flexor and cam extensor are reported in Section~\ref{sec:redesign_physical}.

For the knee-flexor route, the pulley variant converged to a collision-feasible layout with zero bound violations and a peak predicted flexion torque of \qty{85.56}{\newton\meter} on the insertion-side measure, the torque applied at the insertion after the tackle's force loss. At unity transmission, the knee-flexor pulley route increased peak predicted knee-axis torque from \qty{81.23}{\newton\meter} to \qty{85.74}{\newton\meter}, while limiting off-axis torque to \qty{0.803}{\newton\meter}. This comparison measures the effect of the two optimized routing configurations; their different objective functions do not isolate the pulley alone. The pulley serves as a redirect in this configuration rather than a force multiplier.

The robot-body model includes an adductor magnus attachment update to $\mathbf{P}_2=(-0.1357,-0.0929,0.0591)$~m. At this location, the model delivers \qty{34.28}{\newton\meter} of flexion torque, $1.12\times$ the human target of \qty{30.61}{\newton\meter}, and \qty{27.62}{\newton\meter} of adduction torque, $0.82\times$ the human target of \qty{33.60}{\newton\meter}. The OpenSim model retains its eight original moving-path-point vias for rectus-femoris and vastii patella tracking; the candidate pulley routes were not added to those paths, and the model contains no pulley entities. The MuJoCo conversion used for the simulation work predates this attachment update and the pulley batch. Physical tests of the redesigned knee routes are reported in Section~\ref{sec:redesign_physical}.

The attachment-route redesign was extended separately from the knee to all 27 muscle-actuators of the robot-body model and remains work in progress. The first pass reached 19 actuators. All but one selected route violated the feasibility constraints; the exception was the biceps femoris long-head pair, with a worst-angle margin of $+0.03\,\%$. The formulation was then restructured to enforce each route's joint-angle task envelope as a hard feasible set rather than a penalty.

The restructured pass encountered a route-builder concatenation error for twelve actuators, leaving fifteen finalized routes. Eight were constraint-feasible: semimembranosus, biceps femoris long head, tensor fasciae latae, soleus, lateral gastrocnemius, tibialis anterior, iliacus, and psoas. Six also met the torque requirement at every angle, with worst-angle margins from $+0.25\,\%$ for biceps femoris long head to $+250.7\,\%$ for tensor fasciae latae. Semimembranosus and soleus satisfied the geometry but remained below their torque targets. Completing the extension requires the route-builder repair and sufficient actuator capacity or relaxed routing corridors for these two routes.

The unadjusted cam optimizer solution had $s_{\mathrm{req}}=+31.30$\,\% and $m_h=+42.34$\,\%; the adjusted design in Chapter~\ref{ch:torque} has $+30.83$\,\% and $+39.77$\,\%, respectively. Equation~\eqref{eq:cam_summary_definitions} distinguishes surplus normalized by peak human torque from the pointwise human-reference margin.
