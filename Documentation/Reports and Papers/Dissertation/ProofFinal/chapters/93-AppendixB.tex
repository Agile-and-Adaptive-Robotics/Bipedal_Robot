\section{Spiking Neuron Model}

Each spiking neuron is a conductance-based leaky integrator with threshold and adaptation,
\begin{equation}
    C_{m}\,\dot{V} \;=\; -\,g_{\text{leak}}\left(V - E_{\text{rest}}\right)
                      \;+\; I_{\text{syn}} \;+\; I_{\text{app}},
    \label{eq:app_membrane}
\end{equation}
where $V$ is the membrane potential, $C_{m}$ the membrane capacitance ($g_{\text{leak}} = C_{m}/\tau_{m}$ with membrane time constant $\tau_{m}$), $E_{\text{rest}}$ the resting potential, $I_{\text{syn}}$ the summed synaptic current, and $I_{\text{app}}$ any applied (tonic stimulation) current. A spike is emitted when $V$ exceeds the firing threshold, after which $V$ resets and an after-hyperpolarization (AHP) of amplitude $A_{\text{AHP}}$ with time constant $\tau_{\text{AHP}}$ is applied; the threshold itself accommodates with the firing history (relative accommodation $r_{\text{acc}}$, time constant $\tau_{\text{acc}}$). Optional calcium channels are defined but disabled in the archived AnimatLab model ($G_{\text{max,Ca}} = 0$).

The parameter values shared by the neurons in the walker model are given in Table~\ref{tab:app_neuron}.

\section{Non-Spiking (Graded) Chemical Synapses}

Pattern-formation and motoneuron-drive connections in the walker use graded chemical synapses, whose conductance is a clipped linear ramp of the presynaptic potential,
\begin{equation}
    g_{\text{syn}}(V_{\text{pre}})
    \;=\; g_{\text{max}}\;
    \operatorname{clamp}\!\left(
        \frac{V_{\text{pre}} - V_{\text{th}}}{V_{\text{sat}} - V_{\text{th}}},
        \,0,\,1\right),
    \qquad
    I_{\text{syn}} = g_{\text{syn}}\left(E_{\text{syn}} - V_{\text{post}}\right),
    \label{eq:app_nonsyn}
\end{equation}
Here inward current is positive, consistently with Equation~\eqref{eq:app_membrane}; an excitatory reversal potential above the postsynaptic voltage therefore produces depolarization. The quantities $V_{\text{th}}$ and $V_{\text{sat}}$ are the synaptic threshold and saturation potentials and $E_{\text{syn}}$ the reversal potential. The walker model instantiates three non-spiking synapse types:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Nicotinic ACh (excitatory drive):}
          $E_{\text{syn}} = \qty{-10}{\mV}$,
          $g_{\text{max}} = \qty{0.5}{\micro\siemens}$,
          $V_{\text{th}} \to V_{\text{sat}} = \qty{-65}{\mV} \to \qty{-20}{\mV}$.
    \item \textbf{Hyperpolarizing IPSP (inhibition):}
          $E_{\text{syn}} = \qty{-70}{\mV}$,
          $V_{\text{th}} \to V_{\text{sat}} = \qty{-55}{\mV} \to \qty{-40}{\mV}$.
    \item \textbf{Depolarizing IPSP:}
          $E_{\text{syn}} = \qty{-55}{\mV}$,
          $V_{\text{th}} \to V_{\text{sat}} = \qty{-60}{\mV} \to \qty{-40}{\mV}$.
\end{itemize}

Spiking chemical synapses and electrical synapses (rectifying coupling weight 0.1, non-rectifying 0.2) are also defined in the model and are available for the sensory afferent pathways of the MuJoCo/SNS implementation.

\section{Relation to the SNS-Toolbox Implementation}

SNS-Toolbox implements corresponding non-spiking and spiking conductance-based neurons and synapses \citep{nourse_sns_2023}. The AnimatLab parameters initialized a 410-neuron SNS-Toolbox network with per-leg rhythm-generator half-centers, phase-shifted pattern-formation groups, motoneuron pools for all 92 muscle-tendon actuators, and Ia, Ib, and II afferent populations. Its non-spiking equations have the forms of Equations~\eqref{eq:app_membrane} and~\eqref{eq:app_nonsyn}, with spiking threshold and accommodation removed. Afferent resting-tone offsets are zero, so Ia and Ib encode activity without a tonic offset.

The rhythm-generator layer oscillated under tonic drive with the legs in antiphase (Section~\ref{sec:spiking_methods}). The layer-specific tests apply constant $I_{\text{app}}$ at MN, PF, or RG cells. The matched circuit checks in Chapter~\ref{ch:sns} concern selected implementations and do not establish equivalence of the larger networks.

\subsection{Adapting Integrate-and-Fire Cells and Hybrid Synapses}

Between spikes, every spiking cell of the MuJoCo mirror obeys the membrane equation
\begin{equation}
    C_{m}\,\dot{V} \;=\; -\,g_{m}\left(V - V_{\text{rest}}\right)
        \;+\; I_{\text{syn}} \;+\; I_{\text{bias}},
    \label{eq:app_lif}
\end{equation}
A spike occurs at threshold $\theta$, after which $V$ resets to $V_{\text{reset}}$ and $\theta$ increases by $\Delta\theta$. Between spikes, $\theta$ decays toward its initial value with time constant $\tau_{\theta}$, bounded by the adaptation floor. Tonic bias $I_{\text{bias}}$ places the equilibrium below threshold. Table~\ref{tab:app_spk_cells} gives the values used by SNS-Toolbox's \texttt{SpikingNeuron}; this archived implementation uses $g_m=\qty{1}{\micro\siemens}$ throughout to retain consistency with its specified current units.

The rhythm-generator adaptation time constant is inherited from the analog persistent-sodium inactivation value, \qty{0.35}{\second} at the rhythm test's operating point. In the contact-driven analog cores, that slowly inactivating inward current terminates bursts. Its inactivation time constant is fixed in the tested implementations because the voltage-dependent form quenched termination and left the network latched.

Spiking chemical synapses, in both the toolbox class used in the MuJoCo mirror and the Simulink \texttt{SpikingSynapse} block, carry a conductance that decays between events and jumps by a fixed increment per presynaptic spike, saturating at its maximum,
\begin{equation}
    \dot{g} \;=\; -\,g/\tau_{\text{syn}} \;\;\text{between spikes}, 
    \qquad g \leftarrow \min\!\left(g_{\max},\, g + g_{\text{inc}}\right) \;\;\text{per spike},
    \label{eq:app_spk_syn}
\end{equation}
with $I_{\text{syn}} = g\,(E_{\text{syn}} - V_{\text{post}})$ as for the graded synapse of Equation~(\ref{eq:app_nonsyn}). The Simulink \texttt{HybridSpikingSynapse} implements the same dynamics with the spike detected from the presynaptic voltage crossing a detection threshold that must sit strictly between the presynaptic resting and firing potentials (a detection threshold equal to the firing potential fires nothing, because the presynaptic voltage resets at threshold). Reversal potentials are \qty{0}{\mV} (excitation, above rest) and \qty{-70}{\mV} (inhibition, shunting at rest) in the spiking frame, and $+8$/$-5$~mV in the motor neurons' 0--5~mV frame. The graded-to-spiking command pathways (descending drive, posture, and bias surrogates) keep the graded synapse of Equation~(\ref{eq:app_nonsyn}).

\subsection{Calibration of Record}

The spike-to-motor-neuron factors are bisected so that a steady \qty{40}{\hertz} presynaptic train reproduces the non-spiking steady-state motor-neuron voltage; the spike-to-spiking factors are rate-calibrated (a \qty{40}{\hertz} source drives a relay cell at \qty{40}{\hertz}, and a calibrated inhibitory factor silences a \qty{40}{\hertz}-driven cell); the readout-tap factor maps a \qty{50}{\hertz} train to the full-scale analog level. The rhythm-generator threshold increment was set by an isolated-pair sweep followed by a full-network sweep over alternation quality and period target.

\section{Connectome of the Verified W2L Model}\label{app:connectomes}
\graphicspath{{../Figures/93-AppendixB/}{figs/93-AppendixB/}}

Figures~\ref{fig:app_circuit_full} and~\ref{fig:app_circuit_core} document the W2L configuration used for the independent replays in Section~\ref{sec:app_supported_replays}. The corrected bilateral template compiles to 96 neurons, 204 chemical synapses, and 22 input channels. The node and connection lists were read from the compiled SNS-Toolbox network using the saved configuration \nolinkurl{w2l_verified_config.json}. This is a different network from the 410-neuron Gait2392 model used in the earlier spiking comparisons; their neuron counts and results are not interchangeable.

The two sides each contain 48 neurons. RG denotes rhythm-generator cells; RG-IN, their local interneurons; PF, pattern-formation cells; PF-IN, their local interneurons; MN, motor neurons; RC, Renshaw cells; Ia, Ib, and II, muscle-afferent encoders; and Ia-IN, reciprocal-inhibition interneurons. The c1 and V3 commissural interneuron populations follow the bilateral organization of \citet{shinohara_mechanisms_2025}: c1 mediates inhibitory coupling between the flexor rhythm generators, while V3 excites contralateral extensor interneurons. Contact comprises heel and toe encoders. The motor outputs drive six actuator channels per side. External inputs and actuator outputs are interfaces, not additional neurons.

Figure~\ref{fig:app_circuit_full} groups individual connections by their presynaptic and postsynaptic populations. A symbol indicates at least one connection of that sign; a population pair can contain both excitatory and inhibitory connections. The diagonal quadrants contain within-side pathways, and the other quadrants show interleg pathways. The matrix exposes the sensory projections to both RG and PF populations as well as to motor circuitry.
