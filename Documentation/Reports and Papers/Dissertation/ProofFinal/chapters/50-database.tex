\chapter{The Sensory Afferent Database}\label{ch:database}
\graphicspath{{../Figures/50-database/}{figs/50-database/}}

The Sensory Afferent Database (SADb) is a curated literature resource for studying sensory feedback in locomotion and posture. It connects papers to afferent classes, experimental preparations, feedback pathways, and computational models. I developed it within the AARL's NeuroNex Communication, Coordination, and Control in Nervous Systems (C3NS) effort. The resource and the evaluation of AI-assisted literature curation were presented at Neuroscience 2023 and 2024, respectively \citep{bolen_sensory_2023,bolen_using_2024}.

SADb addresses the need to recover measured afferent properties, feedback pathways, and experimental conditions from a dispersed literature. Reviews provide overviews of spinal and proprioceptive feedback \citep{van_de_crommert_review_1998, windhorst_muscle_2007, windhorst_spinal_2022}. SADb brings individual studies into a searchable collection so that a researcher can compare experimental preparations, locate measurements relevant to a candidate feedback pathway, and follow each record to its source.

The primary deliverable is SADb Explorer, a browser application containing 1,393 literature and related records. It has four main tabs (Figure~\ref{fig:sadb_clusters}, callout~(A)): Table searches individual records, Pivot compares classifications, Bubble map explores citation links among papers, and Search (online) finds candidate additions through OpenAlex. The records, notes, and citation graph are embedded in the application, so the first three tabs can be used without an internet connection. Search (online) requires an internet connection and lets the user save candidate records for later curation. The application preserves both the records retained for active curation and archived records from the collected material.

The database was curated in Airtable, an online database system, before being transferred to SADb Explorer. Linked tables connect papers to feedback pathways and computational models, with separate records for reviews and recording methods. Paper records contain bibliographic information, curation notes, and classification tags; pathway and model records link these sources to candidate feedback mechanisms and their implementations.

\section{Curation Methods}\label{sec:sadb_methods}

This was a curated research collection, rather than a registered systematic review or an exhaustive search of every relevant publication. I first collected records from selected AARL and personal Zotero collections used for previous papers on locomotion and postural balance, together with a few records already curated in Airtable by my advisor, Dr. Hunt. Duplicate Zotero entries describing the same publication were combined into a single record using DOI and title matching. I expanded the collection through keyword searches, references cited by relevant papers, and searches for subsequent papers citing them. Reviews of spinal and proprioceptive feedback guided the search for primary studies \citep{van_de_crommert_review_1998, windhorst_muscle_2007, windhorst_spinal_2022}.

I initially imported the broader Zotero collections as groups, then narrowed the working base to sensory feedback in locomotion and posture. I retained records reporting sensory-afferent measurements, investigating feedback in locomotion or posture, or modeling a relevant sensory pathway. I removed records outside this scope from the working base. I also flagged records whose source text I could not locate, including conference posters found through ResearchRabbit, and removed them from that base. The application preserves the broader collection as archived records, which users can distinguish with the archive filter. For retained papers, I recorded experimental preparation, afferent class, feedback pathway, and the mechanism described by the study.

\section{Results: Application and Corpus Coverage}\label{sec:sadb_corpus}

The application contains 1,393 entries in five record categories: 943 Papers, 247 Models, 157 Review Papers, 42 Feedback pathways, and 4 Recorders. The searchable table includes the complete 1,393-entry collection. The Pivot and Bubble map tabs use the 943 entries in the Papers category. Archived records remain searchable in the application.

Within the application's Papers category, containing 943 entries, 833 carry curation notes, 606 carry animal-preparation tags, 316 carry animal-study assessments, 119 link to feedback pathways, 95 carry an afferent classification, and 363 carry robot-or-simulation translation notes. DOI identifiers are present for 892 papers, and 318 have attached-PDF indicators. Within the Papers category, 730 papers are connected by 11,130 directed citation pairs. The Louvain algorithm groups these papers by their citation connections into 18 named topic clusters, which help users navigate related studies in the Bubble map tab.

The Table tab combines text search with filters for preparation, pathway, afferent class, record type, archive status, year, and note or PDF availability (Figure~\ref{fig:sadb_explorer} in Appendix~\ref{app:sadb_records}). Selecting a record opens its source information, curation notes, and notes on how the reported findings could inform a robot or simulation. The Search (online) tab queries OpenAlex for candidate additions and marks DOIs already represented in SADb.

The Bubble map tab offers two selectable layouts (Figure~\ref{fig:sadb_clusters}). The year--citations layout places publication year on the horizontal axis and citation count on the vertical axis. The topic landscape places papers using a spring layout of the citation network, so position reflects citation connections rather than publication year or citation count. The user controls the layout, display style, record grouping, citation-neighborhood depth, and click action (Figure~\ref{fig:sadb_clusters}, callout~(B)). When the user selects a paper, the application identifies it as the focus in the left record area (Figure~\ref{fig:sadb_clusters}, callout~(C)), which exposes its curation note and notes on possible applications to a robot or simulation (Figure~\ref{fig:sadb_clusters}, callout~(D)). The neighborhood selector displays papers up to one, two, or three citation links from the focus, following either papers cited by the focus record or papers that cite it (Figure~\ref{fig:sadb_clusters}, callout~(E)). A two-link neighborhood adds one level beyond the directly connected papers. Directly connected papers retain color, while papers reached through additional links are gray. For example, in the illustrated Grillner (1985) neighborhood, 57 in-corpus papers cite the focus, the focus cites 13 in-corpus papers, and 555 papers lie within two links. Green triangles identify papers that cite the focus record, and red circles identify papers cited by it.

The connection list separates papers citing the focus from its references (Figure~\ref{fig:sadb_clusters}, callout~(F)). Selecting a listed paper makes it the new focus, allowing a reader to follow the development of an idea without repeatedly searching for individual titles. The reference-only and cited-by views isolate either direction, and the detail action opens the underlying record. A researcher can therefore start from a known experiment, inspect earlier studies supporting it, follow subsequent models or experiments citing it, and read the associated translation notes before choosing a candidate pathway for simulation.

The Pivot tab counts papers by two selected dimensions, including topic cluster, decade, primary author, animal preparation, feedback pathway, and afferent classification. Figure~\ref{fig:sadb_pivot} crosses afferent classes with animal preparations: each cell counts papers carrying both tags. Rows and columns can be ordered alphabetically or by count. Selecting a cell opens the corresponding records in the Table tab, and a return control restores the pivot. The pivot is computed over the 943 entries in the Papers category, including archived entries, rather than all 1,393 records. Because a paper can carry several animal or afferent tags, it can contribute to several cells. The counts describe the distribution of curated records, not independent experimental observations or the strength of evidence for a pathway.

The feedback-pathway records extend beyond the mammalian core of Table~\ref{tab:sadb_pathways}: paired excitation and inhibition records cover the chordotonal organs and trochanteral hair plates of insect legs, a load-feedback record covers the campaniform sensilla, and further records hold fictive locomotion without sensory feedback (12 linked papers), group III/IV fatigue, and one paper each for somatosensory, vestibular, and visual contributions to postural control. The controller of Chapter~\ref{ch:sns} implements the mammalian subset.

\subsection{AI-Assisted Literature Triage}\label{sec:sadb_triage}

I evaluated AI-assisted literature identification, summarization, and categorization. This work was presented at Neuroscience 2024 \citep{bolen_using_2024}. The comparison includes ResearchRabbit, Litmaps, txyz, SciSpace, and MirrorThink. Some tools helped identify related papers or summarize straightforward material, but categorization remained inadequate: none received a good rating, and only txyz was rated adequate. Complex models and nuanced physiological findings required substantial human correction. The tools therefore offered limited help with the central task of accurately assigning papers to database categories. I retained responsibility for inclusion, classification, and deletion decisions after checking the source.

\section{Discussion}
\subsection{Uses of the Database}\label{sec:sadb_use}

SADb helps researchers connect an account of sensory feedback to the experiments and models supporting it. Searchable notes, linked records, and citation neighborhoods provide complementary routes through a literature spread across preparations and disciplines. A reader can begin with a familiar paper, follow its references and later citing studies, and compare the conditions under which a feedback mechanism was observed. This makes the resource useful for learning a research area and for identifying questions that require a closer reading of the primary evidence.

The database also supports the design of neural models for locomotion and balance. Its afferent and pathway records connect candidate sensory signals, connection signs, and phase dependence to cited experiments (Tables~\ref{tab:sadb_afferents} and~\ref{tab:sadb_pathways}). A modeler can use these links to distinguish a measured response from an assumption needed to implement it. Model records provide further starting points through two-level rhythm generation and pattern formation \citep{rybak_modelling_2006}, muscle-reflex walking \citep{geyer_musclereflex_2010}, and the laboratory walker lineage \citep{deng_neuromechanical_2019}. For the controllers in Chapter~\ref{ch:sns}, these records inform pathway selection; receptor dynamics, gains, delays, and species-specific assumptions still require explicit selection and testing.

The balance, posture, and multisensory-recalibration clusters provide entry points for the vestibular and visual extensions proposed in Section~\ref{sec:fw_senses}. Preparation-based searches also let new users approach the literature through the experiment they intend to perform. The value of these connections is that a researcher can compare the preparation and task behind a proposed feedback rule before transferring it to a different model.

\subsection{Further Improvements}

The application combines searchable notes, classification comparisons, and citation navigation, providing a foundation for further curation. Classification remains incomplete within the application's 943-entry Papers category: 95 carry an afferent classification, and 119 link to named pathways. Completing these fields would improve comparisons across preparations and help expose where a proposed pathway depends on a small or indirect body of evidence. Missing source text should remain explicit until the paper can be checked; model and review records should link to the primary experiments supporting their assumptions.

Further curation should connect pathway records to the papers that establish their signals, connection signs, and task dependence, and connect model records to the pathways they implement. Vestibular and visual contributions to posture require expanded classifications and source coverage before the database can support the additional sensory channels proposed in Section~\ref{sec:fw_senses}. New records should include a source identifier and a substantive curation note, with incomplete classifications flagged for review. These improvements would make the resource more useful for selecting experiments and constructing testable neural models.

The application makes physiological evidence accessible for controller design, but selecting a pathway is only the beginning of a neuromechanical experiment. Its encoding, connection sign, gain, and interaction with the body must still be implemented and tested. Chapter~\ref{ch:sns} develops the models and simulation tools needed to make that next step explicit.
