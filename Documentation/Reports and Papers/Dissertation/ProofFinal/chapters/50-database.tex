\section{The Resource}\label{sec:sadb_resource}

The Sensory Afferent Database (SADb) is a curated literature resource for studying sensory feedback in locomotion and posture. It connects papers to afferent classes, experimental preparations, feedback pathways, and computational models. I developed it within the AARL's NeuroNex Communication, Coordination, and Control in Nervous Systems (C3NS) effort and presented the resource at Neuroscience 2023 \citep{bolen_sensory_2023}.

When I began this work, selecting sensory feedback for the robot required finding measured afferent properties and feedback pathways across a dispersed literature. SADb addresses the need to organize those findings by their experimental conditions and potential role in a controller. Reviews organized parts of this evidence \citep{van_de_crommert_review_1998, windhorst_muscle_2007, windhorst_spinal_2022}, but selecting a feedback rule still required recovering its experimental conditions and separating measurements from modeling assumptions. I built SADb to connect those findings to their sources, afferent classes, and pathway roles so that controller choices could be traced to the evidence and remaining unknowns could be identified.

The primary deliverable is a browser application for searching paper records, counting them by selected classifications, and exploring citation neighborhoods (Figure~\ref{fig:sadb_clusters}). Its embedded data support offline use; its separate online-search tab requires a network connection. The distributed snapshot contains 1,393 records: 943 papers and 450 linked model, review, feedback-pathway, and recorder records. The application includes 436 records outside the smaller Airtable working base, so the operating cap does not limit access to the collected literature. The pivot and citation views operate on papers; the table view searches across record types.

The working base in Airtable contains linked Papers, Feedback, Models, Review Papers, and Recorders tables. Papers hold bibliographic information and curation notes: short summaries of the reported mechanism, experimental conditions, and relevance to feedback design, grounded in the source text. Feedback records describe a sensory pathway, such as Ib feedback influencing the stance-to-swing transition, and link it to supporting papers; Models link computational implementations to their source literature. The other tables organize review literature and recording methods. Paper matching uses DOI where available and normalized titles otherwise. The paper-table view is shown in Appendix~\ref{app:sadb_records} (Figure~\ref{fig:sadb_explorer}).

\section{Curation Methods}\label{sec:sadb_methods}

The initial collection drew on the literature libraries used for previous AARL papers on locomotion and postural balance, together with records already curated in Airtable. Records were reconciled by DOI and title, then expanded through citation searches. Reviews of spinal and proprioceptive feedback supplied an initial organization of the literature \citep{van_de_crommert_review_1998, windhorst_muscle_2007, windhorst_spinal_2022}. This was a curated research collection, rather than a registered systematic review or an exhaustive search of every relevant publication.

Records were retained when they reported sensory-afferent measurements, investigated feedback in locomotion or posture, or modeled a relevant sensory pathway. Reviews were retained as sources for organizing and locating that evidence. Duplicate records and material outside this feedback scope were removed from the working base. Classification used controlled vocabularies for preparations and afferent classes. Each mechanism summary was grounded in available source text, using the abstract when full text was unavailable; missing text was flagged rather than supplied from recollection.

\section{Results: Corpus and Curation Coverage}\label{sec:sadb_corpus}

At the close of the curation effort the working base held 957 records: 561 papers, 39 feedback-pathway records, 244 model records, 109 review records, and 4 recorder entries, 43 records below the laboratory's 1,000-record operating cap, with room to grow. Coverage of the retained papers is concentrated where the laboratory's design and experimental work needs it. Of the 561 retained papers, 523 carry curation notes, 426 carry animal-preparation tags, 240 carry notes assessing each paper's animal-study potential, 118 are linked to named feedback pathways, 86 carry an afferent classification, and 258 carry notes translating the paper's findings toward robot or simulation use; 550 carry DOIs, and 38 remain bare pending a findable text.

The citation graph covers all 943 papers in the application, including the 382 papers outside the Airtable working base. Within this corpus, 730 papers are connected by 11,130 directed citation pairs. A Louvain community partition resolves 18 named topic clusters, including afferent locomotor control, spinal interneuron circuit modeling, human balance and posture, bioinspired flow sensing, and deep-learning methods. Of the 943 papers, 833 carry curation notes and 318 have attached PDFs. The application distributes the corpus, citation graph, cluster assignments, and a flat export together. Figure~\ref{fig:sadb_clusters} shows the citation map, in which each paper is drawn as a neuron with soma size proportional to citation count. Appendix~\ref{app:sadb_records} shows the table view (Figure~\ref{fig:sadb_explorer}).



The explorer supports three complementary ways of examining the saved corpus (Figure~\ref{fig:sadb_clusters}A). The table view searches titles, authors, years, DOIs, and curation notes, and combines filters for animal preparation, feedback pathway, afferent type, record type, archive status, year range, and the availability of notes or a PDF. Selecting a record opens its detailed notes, classification tags, DOI link, animal-study assessment, and robot-or-simulation translation. This connects a literature search to the observations and modeling choices recorded during curation. The table includes papers and the linked model, review, feedback-pathway, and recorder records; the pivot and citation-map views operate on papers. These three views use the embedded snapshot offline. The separate online-search tab queries OpenAlex and identifies returned DOIs already represented in the snapshot; suggested additions are downloaded for later curation.

The bubble map supports both publication-year versus citation-count axes and a topic landscape based on the citation-network layout. Its controls select the layout, display style, record grouping, citation-neighborhood depth, and click action (Figure~\ref{fig:sadb_clusters}B). Selecting a paper as the focus identifies it in the left panel (C), which exposes its curation note and proposed robot-or-simulation translation (D). The plotted neighborhood can extend through one, two, or three citation links in either direction (E). Direct connections retain color, while more distant papers are gray. In the illustrated Grillner (1985) neighborhood, 57 in-corpus papers cite the focus, the focus cites 13 in-corpus papers, and 555 papers lie within two links. Green triangles identify incoming citations and red circles identify the focus paper's references. These synapse-like symbols encode bibliographic direction; they do not assert excitatory or inhibitory physiological effects.

The connection list separates papers citing the focus from its references (Figure~\ref{fig:sadb_clusters}F). Selecting a listed paper makes it the new focus, allowing a reader to follow the development of an idea without repeatedly searching for individual titles. The reference-only and cited-by views isolate either direction, and the detail action opens the underlying record. A researcher can therefore start from a known experiment, inspect earlier studies supporting it, follow subsequent models or experiments citing it, and read the associated translation notes before choosing a candidate pathway for simulation.

\subsection{AI-Assisted Literature Triage}\label{sec:sadb_triage}

I evaluated AI-assisted literature identification, summarization, and categorization in work presented at Neuroscience 2024 \citep{bolen_using_2024}. The comparison included ResearchRabbit, Litmaps, txyz, SciSpace, and MirrorThink. Some tools helped identify related papers or summarize straightforward material, but categorization remained inadequate: none received a good rating, and only txyz was rated adequate. Complex models and nuanced physiological findings required substantial human correction. The tools therefore offered limited help with the central curation task of accurately assigning papers to database categories. I retained responsibility for inclusion, classification, and deletion decisions after checking the source.

\section{Discussion}\subsection{Robot and Simulation Use}\label{sec:sadb_use}

The database exists to feed the sensorium of Chapter~\ref{ch:sns}. Its pathway records enumerate the signal taxonomy the controller's sensory channels implement: Ia afferents carrying length and velocity, Ib afferents carrying force, II afferents carrying length, and contact receptors marking the stance--swing boundary (Section~\ref{sec:preliminary_sim_methods}); Table~\ref{tab:sadb_afferents} in Appendix~\ref{app:sadb_records} summarizes what the corpus establishes for each class. The pathway naming grammar maps directly onto circuit edges, because a record identifies a candidate sensory signal, gating phase, and connection sign. Receptor dynamics, gains, delays, and species-specific assumptions still require explicit selection and testing; Table~\ref{tab:sadb_pathways} lists the principal pathway records with the papers that demonstrate them. The Models table carries the lineage the controller instantiates, from the two-level rhythm-generator and pattern-formation architecture \citep{rybak_modelling_2006} through muscle-reflex walking \citep{geyer_musclereflex_2010} to the two-layer neuromechanical walker the laboratory's controller descends from \citep{deng_neuromechanical_2019}, and the 258 robot-and-simulation translation notes exist precisely to shorten the step from paper to wiring decision. Figure~\ref{fig:sadb_pivot} shows the two-dimension count the explorer's pivotable tables produce over the full paper snapshot, the afferent classes against the animal preparations: the classical cat preparation carries the largest share of the Ia, Ib, II, and cutaneous records, and the arthropod records enter through the mechanosensory class.



The pivot view counts papers by two selected dimensions, including topic cluster, decade, primary author, animal preparation, feedback pathway, and afferent classification. Figure~\ref{fig:sadb_pivot} crosses afferent classes with animal preparations: each cell counts papers carrying both tags. Rows and columns can be ordered alphabetically or by count. Selecting a cell opens the corresponding records in the table view, and a return control restores the pivot. The pivot is computed over all 943 papers in the embedded snapshot, including archived papers; it is not restricted to the 561 retained Airtable papers. Because a paper can carry several animal or afferent tags, it can contribute to several cells. The counts describe the distribution of curated records, not independent experimental observations or the strength of evidence for a pathway.

The 39 pathway records extend beyond the mammalian core of Table~\ref{tab:sadb_pathways}: paired excitation and inhibition records cover the chordotonal organs and trochanteral hair plates of insect legs, a load-feedback record covers the campaniform sensilla, and further records hold fictive locomotion without sensory feedback (12 linked papers), group III/IV fatigue, and one paper each for somatosensory, vestibular, and visual contributions to postural control. The controller of Chapter~\ref{ch:sns} implements the mammalian subset.

The corpus also reaches ahead to the sensorium extensions planned in Section~\ref{sec:fw_senses}. The human balance and posture cluster and the multisensory recalibration cluster are the entry points for the vestibular and ocular feedback work, already curated and already connected to the locomotor core through the citation graph. The knowledge base makes this reach practical for new students as well: the cluster, pathway, and animal pages compress a literature that took months to curate into an onboarding document measured in pages, and the animal pages let an experimentalist start from a preparation rather than a topic.

\subsection{Further Improvements}

Classification remains incomplete. Only 86 of the 561 papers in the Airtable working base carry an afferent classification, and 118 link to named pathways. Completing these fields would improve comparisons across preparations and expose where a proposed pathway depends on a small or indirect body of evidence. Missing source text should remain explicit until the paper can be checked; a model or review should link to the primary experiments supporting its assumptions.

Further curation should connect pathway records to the papers that establish their signals, connection signs, and task dependence, and connect model records to the pathways they implement. Vestibular and visual contributions to posture require expanded classifications and source coverage before the database can support the additional sensory channels proposed in Section~\ref{sec:fw_senses}. New records should include a source identifier and a substantive curation note, with incomplete classifications flagged for review. These improvements would make the resource more useful for selecting experiments and constructing testable neural models.
