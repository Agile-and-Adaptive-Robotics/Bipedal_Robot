# Dissertation review from a mechanical design and experimental validation perspective

The equations, Figure 4.9, the Results opening, and the two biomimetic comparisons identified below were corrected in the dissertation. Page references below identify the proof the reviewer read. The [final change record](REVIEW.md) gives the delivered pagination.

The dissertation supports its central mechanical conclusion. The new force characterization improved the estimate of actuator force. Corrections identified on a pinned knee improved torque predictions on a biomimetic knee. The redesigned extensor and flexor then exceeded the respective single-muscle human references at the knee angles actually measured. The pointwise prediction across a continuous design range and the physical measurements at discrete poses are distinguished in Chapters 4 and 8.

I read the full dissertation from the title page through Appendix E, including the bibliography. I cross-checked selected high-consequence claims against identification results, measurement records, route-optimization output, and simulation trial descriptions. I rechecked the revised Chapter 5 and Appendix E in the 326-page full proof. Page numbers below are printed dissertation page numbers in that proof. The mechanical revisions described below were staged separately for the integrated build.

## Corrections and attribution

1. **The Chapter 4 actuator-length equations dropped the artificial tendon.** Equation (4.5), p. 78, defined the input as the origin-to-insertion musculotendon path but omitted the unloaded tendon length. Equation (4.27), p. 88, explicitly starts from (4.5) and subtracts the wrapping loss, so the omission propagated. Appendix A and the flexor and extensor evaluators subtract the artificial tendon and both end fittings. The staged changes restore that term in both places and define it as zero for a route without a tendon. They also correct the zero-load reference to relative strain of one at 620 kPa. The model outputs already used the implemented calculation. The torque paper has the same two length omissions in `ProofFinal/Main.tex` lines 202 and 345 and already uses the correct starred strain at line 200. This review did not modify the paper. 2. **Dissertation Figure 4.9 had diverged from the paper's Figure 9.** Both use the same six tests. The original trial archive independently reproduces all 113 measured angle–torque pairs and all 113 hybrid pairs in the paper figure to an absolute tolerance of 10⁻¹². Tests 5, 6, and 7 form the training row. Tests 1, 2, and 8 form the validation row, as in Appendix C, Table C12. In panel D, the measurement reaches zero at +41.5° and the hybrid calculation gives 3.99 N m there. The paper's extension replays the original 100 model poses through +35° before extending the torque curves to +41.5°. The staged figure and alt text use the approved paper artwork, including the upper-right legend in panel C and the original plotting colors. 3. **The opening of Chapter 4 Results described the order of comparisons before stating their outcome.** Its staged opening states that corrections identified on the pinned knee improved flexor and extensor predictions on the biomimetic knee. The following sentence explains the evidence for a mechanical correction. Updating the actuator force model improved the pinned-flexor prediction, but the rigid-attachment calculation still overestimated torque magnitude. The same author attribution remains and the numerical results stay in their original tables. An optional further cut would shorten the six-statistic recitations in §§4.2.3–4.2.4. The transfer result and the single-flexor shortfall at deeper flexion are the reader's reason to consult Table 4.8. Two distinct pieces of the advisor's history bear on that editorial judgment. In an October comment, he questioned how readers should interpret FVU above one in tests outside the fit. In tracked edits on October 29, he deleted the biomimetic extensor section's opening description of the bracket centroid and a separate account of difficulties measuring the curved actuator between the condyles. The resulting standard is to state the experimental conclusion first and keep only the details necessary to interpret it. The suggestions in this review are my application of that standard.
