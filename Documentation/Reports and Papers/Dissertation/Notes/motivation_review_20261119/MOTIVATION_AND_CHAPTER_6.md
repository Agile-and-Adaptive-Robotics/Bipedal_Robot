## Proposed last paragraph of Motivation

Replace line 16 of the exported `chapters/10-introduction.tex` with the following paragraph.

```latex
A leg strong enough to bear weight can still buckle if the muscles that straighten the knee respond too weakly or too late. As the knee bends under load, sensory nerves in those muscles signal the stretch to the spinal cord. The spinal cord can recruit the same muscles to resist further bending, changing the knee angle and the stretch the nerves sense next \citep{windhorst_muscle_2007}. On the robot, moving an artificial muscle's attachment can change both the motion a command produces and the feedback the circuit receives. I developed tools to build neural circuits from pathways described in the sensory literature and test them in a human musculoskeletal model and in models based on our robot's mechanical design (Chapter~\ref{ch:sns}). In simulation, I can weaken the stretch feedback and predict how much farther the knee will bend under the same load. Measuring that difference on the physical robot would test whether the reflex provides as much support as the model predicts.
```

The opening takes up the question of torque from the preceding mechanical work. The stretch reflex gives the pathways collected in the sensory database something specific to explain. The return from muscle force to knee motion to a new sensory signal explains what connecting a circuit to a moving body means. The ending supplies a reason to integrate a controller with the physical robot in §1.2, line 26. The model predicts a measurable effect, and the robot would allow that prediction to be tested. Chapter 6 itself tested a sensory pulse at the knee and the effect of feedback on a loaded arm. The proposed comparison at the loaded robot knee is a future experiment.

The paragraph uses the dissertation's existing Windhorst reference for muscle feedback. The four sources from the current line 16 on locomotor circuit architecture and simulation software already appear in Chapter 2, lines 156–160, 193, and 234–240. Software history, graphical editing, and tuning move out of this proposed Motivation paragraph. Objective four and the chapter describe those contributions. Human knee-extension experiments have also measured the reflex contribution to knee torque under imposed stretch. [Mrachacz-Kersting and Sinkjær, 2003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12707748/)

I begin with that walker and show how I adapted its circuit for a later walker that I could test repeatedly in the MuJoCo physics engine.
