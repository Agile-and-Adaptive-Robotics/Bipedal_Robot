# Chapter 6 revision and neuromechanical experiments

## What the two graphical reflex circuits establish

The GUI generated both six-neuron circuits through its Build action. A direct sensory pulse tests transmission through the specified synapses. A separate mechanical perturbation tests whether the muscle stretches and produces sensory current. These experiments have different stimulus origins. Their times are measured from the applied simulation event. The direction of the response can be assessed against published human recordings. The absolute size and time course were not fitted to those recordings. ## Reading the controls and experiments

The frozen-controller comparisons isolate the mechanical substitution. The updated neural training solves for changes in weights with the revised muscle recruitment and sensory inputs. In BiW2Lvar+, increasing sensory return and changing descending drive produced a 1.305 s period at the original weights and a 1.292 s period after refitting. The comparable conditions without sensory return both gave 2.466 s. The sensory and drive effects cannot be isolated from each other by that comparison.

For syn6, reopening a channel several times during one rhythm burst exposed a problem in how the network produced its pattern. Locking S2 until the next extensor burst gave one S2 onset in each of five bilateral cycles, but S3 still reopened. Replaying fixed and adaptive timing under prescribed sensory inputs, repeating the 12-candidate physical tuning, and separately running the coupled body identify where the transfer failed. The ground and swing diagnostics report the original prespecified contact and clearance definitions. Ground loading, foot clearance, and agreement with a human gait reference answer different questions.

The 43 external reference records were compared to the same recorded movement. Nine bilateral walking recordings were usable, along with 32 running records and one predicted walking record. The remaining walking recording lacked a usable left cycle. This was a comparison across references, not 43 independently fitted participant models.

## Pressure-driven robotbody stepping

The 264-neuron controller issued 54 pressure commands on the Gait2392 robot counterpart. The baseline and selected arrangements used the Appendix C sleeve diameters, counts, and transmissions. These included 102 and 138 pneumatic sleeves, respectively. The cable lengths and proximal fixed lengths needed to mount them were estimated on the mirrored geometry. They have not been measured on a built robot. The controller used assumed 40 ms filling and 60 ms venting time constants. It integrated the Chapter 3 pressure–contraction equation at each time step with the current sleeve length. At the contraction stop, available output-cable stretch limited the transmitted force.

All five physical stages were run on both designs, in 520 training evaluations and 152 independent checks. At 50.47% body-weight lift, the selected design produced five right and six left swings that cleared the ground with the opposite foot loaded. All four 16 s trials met that criterion, including a changed starting posture, half the time step, and increased contact stiffness. Mean foot loads in the 5–13 s window were 29.9% and 19.6% of body weight. The horizontal harness force and rotational assistance were 52.85 N and 72.52 N m RMS. The knees remained deeply crouched and fore–aft center-of-mass position changed by 1.1 cm over 16 s. Of the 54 commanded routes, 44 developed force.

At 78.5% lift, a different selected configuration met the entire nominal ground-stepping criterion, including knee timing. Four of eight 1% gain perturbations also passed. Changing the initial knee position, halving the integration step, or stiffening contact preserved the alternating loading but missed a joint-motion requirement. Neither design passed every physical stage across the independent conditions. Removing vertical lift with the guides retained led to a fall in 1.70 s for the baseline and 2.66 s for the selected design.

The 50.47%-lift run separates two limitations that could otherwise look identical. During right stance, the right vastus developed up to 47.8 N at a maximum chamber pressure of 131.5 kPa. At the same recorded lengths, median predicted capacity at 620 kPa was 1.94 kN. The right medial gastrocnemius had zero predicted capacity even at the reference pressure. This identifies a recruitment question for the vastus and a geometry question for the gastrocnemius. The simulation does not establish the change in whole-body motion that would follow either intervention. The two permanent example configurations reproduced every saved array in independent runs.

## Controller comparisons after changing the four human muscle equations

The original four-stage neural fit was repeated for BiW2Lvar+, syn6, the reference circuit (s3k), and the W2L-derived circuit. Each study used 16, 32, 24, and 24 evaluations in stages A–D. Two profiles, BiW2Lvar+ and syn6, met the final offline criterion. Coupled-body simulations independently assessed whether each fitted pattern produced the intended movement. The registered 12-candidate ankle-recruitment search was repeated with both the original and new motor weights. The best coupled syn6 periods were 2.036 s and 1.932 s, respectively, outside 15% of the 1.233 s target. In the source SCONE environment the Di Russo controller reproduced the published gait. In the four-muscle Gait2392 environment the source, older refit, and new diagnostic refit all fell. An independent replay also reproduced all eight arrays of the historical native tumbling trajectory selected by a rejected optimization objective. The archive’s parameter search was not reused to claim walking.

The older gait-library configurations and the later feedback finalist have different motor and sensory settings. With the four revised muscles, the library's intact reference, pruned reference, syn6, and W2L-derived configurations gave 2R/5L, 0R/3L, 5R/0L, and 0R/0L qualifying cycles. Removing three designated Ia gains from the library reference reduced its 2R/5L to 0R/0L. The calculation now checks the actual ground-contact histories on both sides. The older scorer could collapse left-only and no-cycle outputs to the same fallback value. Removing all afferent input in the earlier syn6 condition instead produced 9R/10L. A pathway deletion’s effect depends on its controller and baseline.

## Corrections found through the graphical examples

The graphical editor previously omitted a neuron's output threshold and voltage span when saving a circuit. It now preserves both values through Save, Load, and Build. These are neuron output properties, distinct from a synapse's presynaptic threshold or range. The two motor neurons in the biological knee use a 10 mV output span. Those in the pneumatic arm use 1 mV. Both six-neuron circuits were built with the same callback the graphical Build button invokes. The historical spiking pneumatic arm independently reproduced 1.7775° of sag at 10 s. Its model now runs with the base library supplied in the repository.


## Original-budget human feedback optimization

The four original search budgets were 88, 80, 64, and 48 trials. Every one of the 280 trials had a recorded check that the four replacement muscle equations executed in the coupled model. The top-ranked final-round controller, trial 38, met the cycle and stance criteria at a supplementary drive scale of 0.225814. All trials retained the shared 2.0773 nA drive. Independent 16 s and 22 s runs produced 7R/8L and 11R/7L foot-loading cycles, with right/left stance fractions of 62.7%/58.6% and 55.8%/71.0%. Eight of eight one-percent perturbations retained at least three cycles on each side and seven met the stricter six-cycle and stance criteria. The remaining run gave five right and six left cycles. Of the eleven specified drive levels, 0.20, 0.30, 0.38, and 0.40 met the stricter criterion. At zero supplementary drive this controller gave zero right and five left cycles. The previously fitted gains gave six right and seven left cycles at zero. The 22 s repeat at scale 0.30 gave 13 right and ten left cycles.

The 16 s selected controller’s stance fractions of 62.7% and 58.6% compare with 61% on each side in the human recording. Mean absolute-angle root-mean-square error across six joint curves increased from 17.78° for the frozen controller with the revised muscles to 23.36° after retuning. The new right/left periods were 1.611/1.516 s against the reference’s 1.233/1.213 s. Returning the new controller to the original MuJoCo muscles produced no qualifying loading cycles. These results support a specific improvement in stance fraction and demonstrate the controller’s dependence on actuator mechanics. The comparison uses the same 50 N loading threshold and preserves absolute angles.
