## Pressure and the 54-route CAD controller

- **D.1.3 and D.4.3.** In the paired one-joint fixture, each motor neuron requests tension. An inverse of the characterized static pressure–length map finds a pressure target for its route. Actual chamber pressure follows that target with assumed fill/vent time constants and a delay. The current pressure and current length determine tension. The separate directional term multiplies this tension by a factor based on filtered route velocity. It is an assumed force perturbation of up to ±8% in the selected sensitivity cases. The code does not implement or cite an empirical hysteresis law for that term. A same-pressure, same-length test of the underlying quasi-static characterization gave the same 341.798 N from either direction. [Actuator interface](<D:/GitHub/Bipedal_Robot/Code/MuJoCo_SNS/robot_torso/bpa_neural_coordination/actuator_interface.py>) · [Executable perturbation](<D:/GitHub/Bipedal_Robot/Code/MuJoCo_SNS/robot_torso/bpa_neural_coordination/run_fixture.py:72>) · [Static result](<D:/temp/dissertation_advisor_nov20/appendixD_force_check.json>)
- In the CAD foot-clearance trial the neural circuit set swing phase. The engineering controller computed joint torques from posture and speed error, load still borne by the foot, and desired swing clearance. Each route’s signed moment arms and maximum tension give its torque at unit activation. A bounded least-squares solve allocates the torque demand across the 54 tensile routes, penalizing excessive activation. Target motor current compensates for the background synaptic currents so the MNs express these activations. The route tensions equal the filtered activations times their force capacities. All 54 used reference tension. In a **separate standing trial**, two knee-extensor routes received pressure via the neural interface, while the other 52 retained reference tensions. The force-assisted clearance trial does not establish 54 pneumatic circuits or independent neural selection of all actuator forces.
