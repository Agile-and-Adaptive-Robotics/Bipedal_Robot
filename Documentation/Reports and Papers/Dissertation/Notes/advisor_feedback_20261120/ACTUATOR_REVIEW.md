# Human muscle force and robot pressure actuation

The two human muscles affect joint motion. A paired simulation separated the error in the original force comparison from sensitivity of the controller to a physical change in its muscle actuators.

## Human muscle estimates

The archived 121-frame calculation reported force-agreement scores of −1.15 for rectus femoris and −0.26 for the short head of biceps femoris. The analysis compared total MuJoCo tendon tension at zero joint speed with the active component of force that OpenSim static optimization estimates during motion. It also used a truncated polynomial for a patellar guide and inconsistent physical hip-axis signs. Rectus femoris and the short head had different sources of error. Restoring the fourth-order guide term reduced the rectus error. Removing passive tension from the *comparison* was especially important for the short head. Both active and passive tension still enter the equations of motion.

## Robot actuator model

The published 54-route CAD stepping trial used an engineering controller to calculate joint torque demand. Each route's signed moment arm translates a tension in newtons into a joint torque. The controller allocates simultaneous nonnegative route tensions and the ideal actuators develop them with a 30 ms filter. At 21.61 s the left knee-extensor tension request was 2,528 N. The ideal actuator developed 2,088 N. Applying the single 20 mm BPA configuration used for standing to the archived 0.23906 m route length gives a static capacity of 1,142 N at 620 kPa. This is a fixed-posture comparison between specified configurations. It neither establishes the actual installed hardware capacity nor reruns CAD stepping. [Capacity calculation](../../../../../Code/MuJoCo_SNS/robot_torso/bpa_neural_coordination/cad_tension_capacity.py)

The mirrored Gait2392 robot counterpart runs with pressure-driven BPAs on all 54 assigned routes. It accepts 54 pressure commands or connects all routes to the 256-cell nonspiking controller. Pressure and contraction determine force. The assigned sleeve sizes draw on measured specimens. Installation and pressure dynamics are simulation parameters. With the pelvis fixed and no ground contact, raising one pressure command from 350 to 600 kPa changed the native OpenSim right knee by 5.5° toward extension for vastus medialis and 29.5° toward flexion for short-head biceps relative to the common control at 150 ms. On the MuJoCo conversion the corresponding changes were 0.28° toward extension and 17.1° toward flexion. Compliant stops bounded joint motion. The separate neural comparison began with zero pressure. Chamber pressure followed the motor outputs and all sensory channels were connected. Right-knee extension relative to zero commands was 13.2° in OpenSim and 5.3° in MuJoCo. The native and converted implementations agree on direction and differ in excursion. The short fixed-pelvis tests establish actuation and sensor feedback.
